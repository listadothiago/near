There are exactly two ways into Praia do Bonete, and neither of them is a road. You walk twelve kilometers, or you take a boat. That's it. If a listing tells you it's "just a short trip," it's lying, rounding, or has never done it.

## The trail, honestly

The hiking route runs about 12km and it isn't padding — it passes three named waterfalls along the way: **Cachoeira da Laje**, **Cachoeira do Areado**, and **Saquinho**. That's not incidental scenery, that's the actual structure of the hike: you're not walking to the beach so much as walking through a sequence of stops that happen to end at one. The trail delivers you to a viewpoint overlooking the caiçara community before you drop down into it — the kind of vantage that every hidden-gem listicle screenshots and none of them tell you costs a full day of walking to earn.

The other option is by boat — transfers run by the community itself, or booked through tourism agencies on Ilhabela. If twelve kilometers and three waterfalls sound like a plan and not a punishment, walk it. If they sound like a liability, take the boat and don't feel bad about it. Both are legitimate ways to arrive; only one of them is a hike.

Either way, you're getting to Bonete via Ilhabela first, which itself means a ferry from São Sebastião, on São Paulo's north coast. The trip stacks in layers — ferry, then island, then trail or boat — and most people turn back at one of them toward somewhere easier.

## What's actually there

Bonete is a **traditional caiçara fishing community** — people who live off fishing and, increasingly, tourism, not a beach that got a community built around it for visitors. That distinction matters for what you should expect. There are no cars here, full stop. **Electricity comes from a community generator that runs from 5pm to 11pm**, supplemented by solar panels — outside those hours, or wherever the solar isn't carrying the load, you're in the dark, and that's not a design choice made for ambiance, it's the actual infrastructure. There's no cell signal, and internet is weak satellite service where it exists at all. If you need to be reachable, don't come here expecting to be.

Accommodation ranges wider than the "no signal, no lights past 11" framing might suggest — there are beachfront pousadas describing themselves as rustic yet sophisticated, and simpler homestay-style rooms with local caiçara families. I'm not naming specific properties here; I haven't verified any of them closely enough to vouch for one over another, and a debut piece isn't the place to guess.

The beach itself is roughly 600 meters of clear sand facing the open sea — good swell for surfers, good silence for people who just want to sit. It's not a lagoon and it's not sheltered; it's an open-ocean beach that happens to be very difficult to reach, which is most of what makes it feel undisturbed.

## Who this is actually for

Here's where I'll drop the neutral tour-guide voice, because that's the whole point of this byline. "Hidden gem" gets used for places that are five minutes off a parking lot with a good Instagram angle. Bonete is not that. Getting there costs a full day either as a hike or a boat booking, and once you're there you're committed to a place with no cars, no phone signal, and lights that go off at 11pm sharp, generator willing.

If what you actually want is a remote beach you worked to reach, a community that still runs on fishing rather than performing quaintness for visitors, and an evening where the dark just means it's dark — this is worth the twelve kilometers or the boat fare. If what you want is a "hidden gem" you can post about with full signal and a charged phone, do yourself a favor and pick somewhere fifteen minutes from Ilhabela's main strip instead. Bonete won't punish you for coming with the wrong expectations, but it also won't accommodate them. Bring a paper map, a charged battery bank, and the actual willingness to be somewhere the internet can't easily follow you.

That's this beat: places where access is the content, and I'd rather tell you plainly whether the trek is worth it than sell you a hike you'll resent halfway up Cachoeira da Laje.
