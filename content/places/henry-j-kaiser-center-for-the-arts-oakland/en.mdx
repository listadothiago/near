Facing Lake Merritt, the domed building at 10 10th St has worn several names across a century — Oakland Civic Auditorium, then Kaiser Convention Center — and hosted acts as varied as the [Grateful Dead, James Brown, and Bruce Lee's 1964 karate demonstration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Convention_Center) before closing roughly 20 years ago.

## Five venues in one reopened landmark

[It reopened January 24, 2026](https://abc7news.com/) as the Henry J. Kaiser Center for the Arts, following a $100 million renovation. The building now holds five distinct performance and event spaces under its landmark dome: a full arena, the 1,500-seat Calvin Simmons Theatre, a speakeasy-style lounge, and two ballrooms — a scale of reopening rare for a building that sat dark for two decades.

The relaunch wasn't without friction. [Oaklandside reported](https://oaklandside.org/) a labor dispute between the venue and IATSE Local 107, the stagehands' union, over the staffing contract governing the reopened building — worth knowing for anyone curious about how the venue is actually run day to day, not just what's programmed inside it.

For more of the Lake Merritt area's cultural spots, the venue sits within walking distance of the lake itself and Oakland's broader downtown arts corridor.
