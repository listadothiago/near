Jabaquara station isn't a food destination. It's a transit hub — the end of Line 1, a bus terminal, a Carrefour. Most searches for something good to eat nearby turn up nothing worth the walk. Jóia de Minas is the exception: a rustic, family-run Minas Gerais restaurant eleven minutes on foot from the platform, doing the kind of home cooking that doesn't need a scene to be worth the trip.

## What's actually good here

The house dish, also called Jóia de Minas, stacks pernil, linguiça, mandioca, rice, tutu de feijão and couve on one platter built for up to three people — the kind of full-table spread Minas cooking is built around, not a single hero ingredient with garnish. Torresmo carnudo (meaty pork crackling) and bolinho de mandioca show up as the most-ordered items on [the house's own menu highlights](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Restaurante+J%C3%B3ia+de+Minas/@-23.6399273,-46.6438144,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x94ce5aef01211e87:0x36824e993ff71841!8m2!3d-23.6399273!4d-46.6438144!16s%2Fg%2F1ptzq7x28), which tracks: this is a kitchen working from a real regional repertoire, not a generic "comida caseira" menu padded out with whatever sells.

Picanha runs [about R$92 for a half portion](https://cardapio.menu/restaurants/sao-paulo/joia-de-minas) that comfortably feeds two, per the delivery-menu listing.

Feijoada gets mentioned in 66 of the restaurant's Google reviews — the single most-cited dish, checked September 2026 — with picanha and torresmo close behind. That's not proof of genius technique, but it is proof of a kitchen that's dialed in its core plates over enough years to earn repeat business from people who aren't reviewing it for content.

## Where it's coasting

Not every plate lands. One recent reviewer ordered the vaca atolada — a Minas classic, short ribs braised in cassava broth, meant for two — and got three small pieces mostly bone, swimming in a thin broth that didn't match the dish's reputation. That's a real complaint, not a one-off nitpick, and it's worth knowing before you order it specifically: the platters and the fried items are the strength here, the braises are more of a gamble.

## The practical version

It's at Av. Gal. Valdomiro de Lima, 241, next to the police station and a short block from the Carrefour — 800m via Rua das Perobas, an 11-minute walk that doesn't require crossing anything unpleasant. R$40–100 a person, and the half-portions are genuinely built for two, not a marketing fiction.

It closes at 10pm, and Fridays are the busiest night by a wide margin, so weekday lunch is the calmer bet if you want a table without a wait. The restaurant's own website is currently offline, so Google Maps and its delivery listings are the most current source for hours and menu changes.
