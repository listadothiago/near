A butcher's family taqueria that started nearly sixty years ago in Mexico City now has a second address, and it's a storefront in Flatiron. [El Califa de León opened at 20 W. 23rd St. on August 24, 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/09/mexico-city-s-legendary-taco-stand-finally-opens-full-restaurant-in-flatiron/), its first stop outside Mexico since Michelin gave a taco stand a star for the first time in 2024 — a distinction it lost in the 2025 update but stayed Michelin-recommended for.

The menu didn't get bigger to make the jump. It's the same four tacos: gaonera, bistec, costilla, chuleta, [built on Pat LaFrieda cuts and grilled corn tortillas](https://www.opentable.com/blog/el-califa-de-leon-nyc/), the same recipe that made a butcher-shop counter famous rather than a scaled-up version of it. Mexico City firm Estudio MERO designed the room as a false façade meant to read like an outdoor CDMX street terrace indoors.

## What the reviews actually say

Read past the opening headlines and a real pattern shows up in [Google and Reddit reviews from the first two weeks](https://cititour.com/NYC_News/First-Look-Inside-Michelin-Starred-El-Califa-de-Leon-in-NYC/10964): the meat and tortilla get real praise, but the price does not travel well — a $19, four-ounce taco next to the original's roughly $5 version reads, to more than one reviewer, as the New York tax rather than the New York upgrade. Worth knowing going in, not a reason to skip it: this is a plate to order two of and split, the way <NearLink slug="rabbit-books-and-bar-east-village-nyc">a good East Village counter</NearLink> rewards showing up without a big group and a bigger appetite than the portion.
