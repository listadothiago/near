Two beaches share one headland at the south end of Guarujá, and they could not be less alike. On the eastern side is Praia do Tombo — 856 metres of hard, breaking surf, [the only beach in São Paulo state carrying the Blue Flag](https://costanorte.com.br/cidades/guaruja/praia-do-tombo.html) and, at sixteen consecutive certifications, [the most certified beach in South America](https://www.guaruja.sp.gov.br/praia-do-tombo-conquista-16a-bandeira-azul-consecutiva-e-sedia-evento-nacional-2). Walk around the rock to the west and you get Guaiúba: about six hundred metres of sand, water that mostly just sits there, and somebody teaching a stranger to stand up on a paddleboard.

Same coast, same day, same swell. The headland does all the work.

## Why the flat water is the point

That geography is the entire reason to come here rather than to the more famous beach next door. Guaiúba faces into the shelter of the point, so the ocean arrives already tired, and the beaches that get surf schools are not the beaches that get paddleboard rental. Here the rental happens [on the sand itself](https://naturam.com.br/praia-do-guaiuba-guaruja/) — boards and kayaks, no booking, no studio, no membership.

Guarujá's own tourism portal lists the beach's activities as [canoagem, stand up paddle and contemplative walking](https://portalguaruja.tur.br/atracoes/praia-do-guaiuba/), which is a fairly precise description of what a sheltered cove is good for. It is also, at six hundred metres end to end, [short enough that beginners can be talked through the whole thing](https://doralguaruja.com.br/10-as-melhores-praias-para-fazer-stand-up-paddle-em-guaruja/) without losing sight of where they started.

There is a caveat, and it is the interesting one: Guaiúba does get surf, on a south swell. The cove is protected, not sealed. If the forecast has swell wrapping in from the south, the flat-water day you planned is somebody else's surf day.

## The kiosks, the calçadão, and the ten o'clock problem

The front is built rather than wild — a portuguese-stone calçadão, a run of standardised kiosks doing petiscos, seafood and cold drinks, and the historic casarões behind them that give the bairro its slightly out-of-time feel. The kiosks rent the umbrellas and chairs, which is the practical fact worth planning around.

There are four large kiosks and a smaller one. That is not a lot of shade for a Saturday in a beach city of this size, and the shade goes early. Arrive by ten at a weekend or accept that you brought your own or you sit in the sun. On a weekday in the shoulder season none of this applies and the beach is close to empty, which is the version most people who recommend Guaiúba are actually remembering.

**If you go, get on the water in the first half of the morning.** Not for the light — because that is when the cove is at its flattest, before the afternoon wind puts a chop on it and makes a rented board a considerably worse deal than it was at nine.

## The water itself

Bathing water is the thing people in the Baixada actually argue about, so: CETESB rated Guaiúba própria — suitable — through the 2025/26 season. Across the municipality, [11 of Guarujá's 12 monitored beaches passed](https://jornaldaorla.com.br/noticias/balneabilidade-praias-guaruja-verao-2025/), the best record on the São Paulo coast, with the single exception a roughly one-kilometre stretch of Praia da Enseada in front of Rua Santa Maria, which stayed red.

That is a weekly measurement, not a permanent condition, and it moves after heavy rain the way it does everywhere. Check the current boletim before you swim rather than trusting a page written in September.

## Getting here the interesting way

Guaiúba sits at the far end of something Near is about to write up properly: the protected bike lane that runs the length of the Santos waterfront to the ferry terminal at Ponta da Praia, the balsa across the working channel of Latin America's largest port, and then the ride south through Guarujá. It is a genuinely good route and almost nobody has written it in English.

If you are building the whole coast into one weekend, <NearLink slug="barraca-da-dheia-guaruja">Barraca da Dhéia</NearLink> on Pitangueiras is the other end of Guarujá's beach culture, and the same lane network runs west past Santos into São Vicente, where <NearLink slug="quiosque-da-cris-sao-vicente">Quiosque da Cris</NearLink> has been holding down Praia do Itararé since the eighties. Somewhere in the middle, <NearLink slug="restaurante-almeida-santos">Restaurante Almeida</NearLink> is the Santos lunch that makes the crossing worth doing on a full stomach.
