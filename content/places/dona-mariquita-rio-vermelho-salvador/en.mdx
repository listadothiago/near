Dona Mariquita sits on R. do Meio in Rio Vermelho, a few doors down from where [SAN](/place/san-boate-rio-vermelho-salvador) picks up much later at night. Chef Leila Carreiro opened it 20 years ago with a specific mission: recover Bahian recipes that had drifted out of restaurants entirely and survived only in candomblé terreiro kitchens, Recôncavo home cooking and open-air markets.

## What's actually on the plate

The poqueca (R$220, serves three) is the house's clearest argument — an indigenous technique that predates moqueca, fish or seafood wrapped in banana leaf and slow-cooked, served with acaçá. The Arroz de Hauçá (R$150) layers coconut rice with dried meat and smoked shrimp, an African-rooted dish that rarely shows up on a menu at all. [CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/viagemegastronomia/gastronomia/em-salvador-dona-mariquita-preserva-a-memoria-gastronomica-da-bahia/) calls the restaurant one of the main guardians of Bahia's patrimonial cuisine, and the walls back that up — clay, lace, basketry and candomblé imagery rather than a generic dining-room look.

## Worth knowing before you go

Reviewers consistently rate the moqueca and the Arroz de Hauçá highly, and one practical note comes up often enough to flag: the moqueca doesn't come with farofa automatically, so order it separately if you want it. Open daily from noon to 5pm — this is a lunch-and-afternoon kitchen, not a dinner spot. A second location, Dona Mariquita Casa de Veraneio, runs alongside the original on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/donamariquita/) if the Rio Vermelho room is full.
