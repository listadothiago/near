I chase the layer underneath cities — what stood here before, what was demolished, what survived. Almeida interests me for the opposite reason: here **nothing needed to survive, because nothing ever left.** Same mansion, same name, same function — feeding the city's conversation — since 1932.

[The house's own record](https://www.restaurantealmeida.com.br/) is plain: founded in 1932, the oldest in Santos, in an early-20th-century mansion at number 1, Avenida Ana Costa, Vila Mathias, steps from the centre and the Municipal Theatre. Ninety-odd years of open doors in a port city that tore down and rebuilt nearly everything around it — in my trade, that is a document.

## Who sat at these tables

The patron list the house claims isn't decoration; it's the cultural history of Santos in the form of a clientele: **Patrícia Galvão — Pagu** — the writer and militant who made Santos her final city, the playwright **Plínio Marcos**, the poet **Roldão Mendes Rosa**, trading ideas at these tables. Then the lineage widens: Jamelão, Alcione, Antônio Fagundes, governors, Santos Futebol Clube directors, Port officials — and, by the house's account, **every mayor of Santos**, which makes these tables an informal annexe of city hall.

The Plínio Marcos thread deserves its paragraph, because it's verifiable and it's enormous: [born in Santos in 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pl%C3%ADnio_Marcos), he entered the city's amateur theatre in 1958 under the direct influence of Pagu — the same Pagu of these tables — and that same year wrote *Barrela*, a play banned from the stage for 21 years. Brazil's most censored playwright learned to write the people the theatre ignored here, in this city, in this circuit of tables and talk. When he died in 1999, his ashes were scattered in the Santos ocean. (And yes: I sign PLINIO and I'm writing about Plínio. A coincidence of naming, not a kinship — noted for the record.)

## The house today

No museum-case stasis: since **2016 the house has been run by Marcelo, Márcio and Jayme Muniz Pereira Filho**, with a renovation in 2018. The menu is a Santos classic unashamed of being one — **Filé à Almeida**, bacalhau, camarão, fish, pastas — in portions local tradition treats as a matter of honour: many dishes are sized to share. It is not thesis cooking, like <NearLink slug="made-cozinha-autoral-santos">Madê</NearLink> over in Ponta da Praia; it is continuity cooking, and the two are different merits of the same city.

Open **Monday to Saturday, 11am to 11pm, and Sunday to 10pm** — seven days, which in a ninety-year-old restaurant is close to insolence. Confirm hours before you go, as always; port-city holidays follow their own logic.

## Why go

Because every city has a room where the decisions were made before they became minutes, and Santos's is at number 1, Ana Costa. Sit, order the filet that carries the house's name, and watch: the next table may be arguing about the port, Santos F.C., or a play — same as ninety years ago. Near covers the Baixada from inside, from <NearLink slug="teatro-clube-da-eskyna-santos">Teatro Clube da Eskyna</NearLink> outward, and this is the oldest entry on that list: the restaurant where the city's under-layer is still sitting, having lunch.
