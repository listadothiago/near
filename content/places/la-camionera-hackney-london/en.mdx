A bar tells you what it thinks of itself in the first material choice it makes, and La Camionera's is a set of hand-painted tiles behind the bar that its co-owner made herself. Not commissioned, not sourced from a supplier catalogue — **Clara Solis painted them**, working from Portuguese azulejos and Picasso's Minotaur drawings. That is a different kind of building than most bars bother to be.

## First, the correction: this is Hackney, not Dalston

Get the geography right before anything else, because plenty of listings don't. **La Camionera is at 243 Well Street, Hackney, E9 6RG.** [Dalston Superstore](https://dalstonsuperstore.com/), the venue this bar's whole origin story runs through, is a 25-minute walk away on Kingsland High Street — a different postcode, a different high street, a different night out. Filing this under "Dalston" isn't a rounding error; it sends someone to the wrong side of the borough.

## Built by the people who ran the night, not booked it

**Alex Loveless**, a trans masc musician and DJ, and **Clara Solis**, a Spanish artist, ran [**body2body**](https://dalstonsuperstore.com/event/body2body-3/) at Dalston Superstore — a trans masc night, not a general queer club night, which matters for reading everything that came after. A promoter who books a room learns what a crowd wants for one evening at a time. Someone running a recurring night for the same community learns what that community is missing the other six days.

What they built to answer it started as a pop-up: a fortnightly evening in the basement of a Broadway Market cocktail bar, in February 2024. It went viral in the least metaphorical sense — [practically every queer woman in east London descended on the market for the opening night](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/62084/1/la-camionera-london-lesbian-bar-founders-interview), turning a Wednesday into a street party nobody had scheduled.

## From £80,000 raised to a lease on Well Street

The response funded what came next: **close to £80,000 raised in a few weeks**, enough to take a lease, gut a shopfront on Well Street and open as a full-time venue in **late May 2024**. The founders and press covering the opening called it London's first FLINTA-owned (female, lesbian, intersex, non-binary, trans and agender) lesbian space in over a decade — a claim that lands harder once you know [more than 60% of London's LGBTQ+ venues closed between 2006 and 2022](https://www.wallpaper.com/travel/bars/la-camionera-london-lesbian-bar).

## A room built like the bars Clara grew up going to

The design brief, worked with **Studio Popelo** and **Wet Studio**, was Iberian rather than generically "queer bar": the couple wanted something closer to the café-bars Clara's family frequents near Madrid than to London's louder, darker queer nightlife rooms. That shows up in the choices — brushed aluminium bar, powder-blue flooring, rough plaster walls left exposed after demolition uncovered them, dark wood, cinnamon-red painted ceiling, stained-glass pendant lights, second-hand furniture, a salvaged timber door slid across the staff room entrance.

## Coffee by day, and a real fig tree out back

It also shows up in what the room is for at different hours. Coffee and pastries in the daytime, wine and cocktails after dark — a genuinely testable claim, since the same door is open on both sides of it.

And out back, past windows that open the whole rear wall to the garden: **a fig tree**, whitewashed sculptural seating, rosemary and oleander. It reads less like garden furniture than like the one detail a Madrid corner bar would have that a themed replica wouldn't bother getting right.

## Hours, the walk-in policy, and getting a seat

**Monday and Tuesday, 16:00–23:00. Wednesday and Sunday, 11:00–23:00. Thursday to Saturday, 11:00–23:30.** Happy hour runs daily 16:00–19:00. The venue currently operates **walk-ins only** — the earlier table-booking system has been dropped, per [its own site](https://www.lacamionera.com/), though online listings still floating around from 2024–25 sometimes describe a bookable table that no longer exists.

Walk-in-only at a room this talked-about means what you'd expect: reviewers describe getting in after 3pm on a Saturday as close to impossible, and a Thursday evening reaching capacity by 7pm. The daytime café side is the more testable proposition — a weekday morning coffee, tiles and all, without the queue that builds by early evening.

Service reports are mixed once the room fills — some reviewers describe attentive staff, others describe curt ones on a busy night. That split tracks with the queue pattern above: a small room running walk-in-only at capacity is a harder shift to staff evenly than an empty one.

## Accessibility, and the press since opening

Front-of-house — entrance, bar, main seating, bathrooms — is wheelchair accessible; the rear section and garden require three steps, with a handrail.

The bar has drawn coverage from [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/travel/bars/la-camionera-london-lesbian-bar), [i-D](https://i-d.co/article/la-camionera-hackney-london-lesbian-bar-2024/), [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/62084/1/la-camionera-london-lesbian-bar-founders-interview) and [PinkNews](https://www.thepinknews.com/2024/05/21/new-lesbian-bar-la-camionera-opens-in-london/) since opening — none of it surprising once you've seen the queue for a lesbian café-bar with hand-painted tiles on a Thursday in Hackney.
