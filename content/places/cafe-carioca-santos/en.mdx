No tables, no chairs, no printed check to sign — the whole transaction at Café Carioca happens standing at a counter that has not moved since 24 November 1940. Four partners opened the place a year earlier, on Rua Dom Pedro II, then walked it one block to the corner of [Praça Mauá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pra%C3%A7a_Mau%C3%A1) and never walked it again — the [official history](https://www.cafecarioca.com.br/) and [Juicy Santos's own 85th-anniversary reporting](https://www.juicysantos.com.br/comida/cafe-carioca-85-anos/) agree on both dates.

Eighty-six years later it's still the same corner, the same counter format, and — per the house's own claim, echoed by [Diário do Litoral's coverage of it as a tourist landmark](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/santos/com-o-pastel-mais-famoso-da-cidade-bar-tradicional-vira-ponto/202525/) — arguably the most famous pastel in the city.

## What's actually in the pastel

Skip the debate about which Brazilian city invented the pastel; Carioca's argument is narrower and better: **volume**. The siri (crab) and camarão (shrimp) versions are the ones locals name first, thick-filled enough that the dough is closer to a container than a wrapper. The bolinho de bacalhau on the side plate is the other constant — salt cod, worked and fried, ordered the way regulars order coffee, without needing to check a menu.

None of this is delicate cooking and it isn't trying to be; it's counter food built to be eaten in the five minutes between one bonde (tram) and the next, which is closer to what this square has always been for.

## The photographs on the wall

A pastelaria doesn't usually double as a civic archive, but Carioca's walls do — and this is exactly the kind of local legend Near checked rather than repeated. Two independent records, not one, back the specific names: [Juicy Santos's roundup of Santos restaurants that "tell the city's history"](https://www.juicysantos.com.br/comida/restaurantes-tradicionais-em-santos/) names Getúlio Vargas, Jânio Quadros and Hortência among the venue's documented visitors.

The [Santos Municipal Council's own 2005 press release](https://www.camarasantos.sp.gov.br/cafe-carioca-recebe-placa-comemorativa), written for a commemorative plaque presented to the house that year, independently names the same three plus Nuno Leal Maia and Ary Toledo — a city government's own record, not a marketing page. Where a claim appeared on only one source and couldn't be cross-checked, it didn't make this piece; several names that circulate in less careful write-ups about the venue are left out for exactly that reason.

## What it isn't

Santos already has a food-drink pin for the formal register — <NearLink slug="restaurante-almeida-santos">Restaurante Almeida</NearLink>, a sit-down dining room a few blocks away with its own 1932-founding claim and its own patron list, Pagu and Plínio Marcos among them. Carioca is the other end of the city's eating culture: nobody is getting a table, nobody is running a tab.

The recent [Google reviews](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Caf%C3%A9+Carioca/@-23.9332053,-46.3284465,17z) make clear it isn't uniformly loved — one visitor in 2026 called a specific dish "sem gosto e massudo" (bland and doughy) and flagged the palmito filling as inconsistent, alongside a much larger volume of reviewers naming the pastel de siri and the tradition itself as the reason to go. That split is worth stating plainly rather than smoothing over: a counter running the same format for 86 years will have an off batch some days, and the record shows both.

## Before you go

Praça Mauá sits in Santos's Centro Histórico, walking distance from the Bolsa do Café and the same historic core Near covers from <NearLink slug="teatro-clube-da-eskyna-santos">Teatro Clube da Eskyna</NearLink> outward. Carioca runs live samba on Monday nights as a standing weekly fixture rather than a one-off booking, so it's worth timing a visit around that if live music is the draw rather than the pastel. Confirm current hours before heading over — a counter this old runs on its own local rhythm, not a chain's posted schedule.
