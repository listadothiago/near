There are two ways to run a hotel attached to a nightclub. You can pretend the club is not there, sell the rooms on sea views and let guests find out at one in the morning. Or you can be straight about it and sell to the people who actually want it. Legends, on the Kemptown seafront, is the second kind, and that honesty is most of why it works.

## What the building actually is

Legends is one address doing three jobs at 31-34 Marine Parade: a hotel upstairs, a bar with a large sea-view terrace at ground level, and the Basement Club underneath. It sits in the middle of Brighton's gay village, on the Regency terrace that runs along the front, and it has been a fixture of queer Brighton long enough that "meet at Legends" needs no further directions.

The club runs [Flavour on Fridays with DJ Ben, Fusion on Saturdays with DJ Peter Castle, and Glitter on Sundays with DJ Patch](https://legendsresortbrighton.com/), with Fever on the last Thursday of the month — Peter Castle again, on 70s and 80s disco, soul and funk. The genuinely distinctive thing is not the club, though. It is the **Sunday cabaret at 3.30pm**, a long-running institution with Davina Sparkle, Miss Jason and Dave Lynn among the names that come through, and a rolling cast of newer acts. A Sunday afternoon cabaret is a different proposition from a Saturday night club, and it is the thing here you cannot easily get elsewhere.

One current-status note worth carrying: the place is **under new ownership**, and the bar and terrace have been refurbished — wood panelling, reworked seating. Reviews older than that are describing a room that no longer exists.

## The bit the booking page will not tell you

Now the part a night porter has to say out loud.

The rooms are above a nightclub that runs Friday, Saturday and Sunday until late. Not near one. Above one. If you book here for a quiet weekend by the sea and then ring the desk at half past midnight, the honest answer is that nobody misled you — the club is the entire point of the building. Come for the club and the cabaret, treat the room as the thing that means you do not have to walk home, and it is a genuinely good deal. Come for a restful minibreak and you have booked the wrong hotel, and no amount of sea view fixes that.

Two smaller things from the same side of the desk. "Compact double" means what you think it means; if you want space, pay up to a superior room rather than hoping. And check-in is 3pm with checkout at 11.30am, which is a short window by hotel standards — but there is a 24-hour front desk and luggage storage, so an early arrival or a late train is a solved problem rather than an argument.

## Going

**If you only do one thing here, make it the Sunday cabaret at 3.30pm rather than a Saturday club night** — the club is good and the cabaret is specific, and specific is the rarer thing. The hotel offers singles, compact doubles and superior rooms with sea views, all en-suite with Wi-Fi throughout.

For the wider Kemptown context, Brighton's gay village runs immediately behind this stretch of seafront, which is worth knowing given the city council's decision to move the Pride street party off St James Street from 2025 — the venues on Marine Parade are now the axis that Pride weekend is built around, and this is one of them.

On the accommodation beat, <NearLink slug="hazlitts-london">Hazlitt's</NearLink> in Soho is the opposite trick for the same money: a historic building that sells silence rather than a sound system. Between them they cover most of what a person actually wants from a room in an English city.
