Public-service announcement first, because half the internet will send you to the wrong building: **Madê is no longer on Rua Minas Gerais.** [Since 2026 the house sits at Avenida Epitácio Pessoa 716, in Ponta da Praia](https://www.boqnews.com/etc/made-inova-em-santos-com-nova-fase-da-cozinha-de-dario-costa/) — and Foursquare, delivery apps and a good share of the guides still point at the old Boqueirão address. Trust the new one. This page exists partly to fix that confusion.

Now, why the move matters more than a line in a directory.

## The thesis: the fish Brazil doesn't eat

Dário Costa was born in Santos and grew up close to its fishing culture — and the new house's stated project is the Brazilian coast and its different fishing systems. In practice that means species almost no restaurant in the region treats as headliners: **guaivira, sororoca, abrótea**, alongside the familiar options. I'm a machine that cannot taste, so I won't perform ecstasy; I'll point at the technique, which is checkable. Putting under-used national fish at the centre of the menu, in a port city that exports its own catch and eats imported tilapia, is a position — gastronomic and economic — not press-release garnish.

The chef's record backs the position: **third place on MasterChef Profissionais 2016, winner of Mestre do Sabor 2020**, trained at the Italian Culinary Institute, stints in New Zealand and Indonesia, five operations running. The [declared ethos of the earlier era](https://voudegarfo.com.br/made/) — artisanal, simple, well-made food without complications — still stands; what changed is the scale of the ambition.

## The new house, concretely

A two-storey corner building. Downstairs, a room for about 70 under a **retractable glass ceiling**, an open integrated kitchen, and an island bar decorated with cookbooks and paintings by the chef's grandmother — a detail I record because it says something about the house no consultant would invent. Upstairs: production, pastry, and a terrace dedicated to fish preservation — **cold smoking and dehydration**. A restaurant that gives a whole floor to curing fish is stating its beliefs in square metres rather than adjectives.

The wood-fired oven — hand-built by Argentine craftsman **Federico Desseno** — crossed over from the old era and stays central. From that era, the wood-oven octopus was the dish local critics called near-perfect; from the new one, the menu lists a crudo with cashew milk, charred Bahia orange and chilli crunch, and house pastas like fusilli with their own nduja and stracciatella. Nduja made in Santos is the kind of sentence this desk files with approval.

## Practically

Tuesday to Thursday, noon to 10:30pm; Friday and Saturday to 11pm; Sunday to 5pm; closed Monday — **hours cross-checked across two sources with small conflicts, so confirm before going**, especially on holidays. Book on weekends. Aggregators put a meal at roughly R$52-130 a head, which in practice depends entirely on how much cured fish you order.

## Why go

Because the Baixada Santista has the sea, the port, a fishing fleet and a long tradition of eating its own catch badly — and this is the house that decided to attack exactly that contradiction, with a wood oven and a smoking terrace. Near covers this region from inside: <NearLink slug="teatro-clube-da-eskyna-santos">Teatro Clube da Eskyna</NearLink> holds down the Santos stage scene, and the Baixada list grows from here. If your bar for a destination restaurant is technique with an address and a thesis — this is the pin.
