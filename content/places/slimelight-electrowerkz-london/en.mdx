Start with the thing that will ruin your evening if nobody tells you: **Slimelight is not a weekly night any more.** Search "goth club London" and you'll get a page of listings that all say every Saturday, because that was true for about thirty-five years and nobody updates a listicle. It isn't true now. Outside the Halloween run — still the big one, still the busiest thing in the calendar — [it happens roughly every six weeks](https://i-d.co/article/slimelight-goth-dark-scene-club-halloween-2024/).

So check first. Turning up at Torrens Street on a random Saturday because a travel blog told you to is a genuinely miserable way to spend a night in Angel.

Right. Now the good part.

## What it is, and why the date matters

Slimelight has been running since 1987, which makes it the oldest thing of its kind still standing anywhere. The [venue that houses it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrowerkz) is a late-19th-century industrial warehouse and former steelworks, and in 1987 it was a squat with no electricity and no doors. Entry was three pounds and you brought your own drink. What it offered was not a superior sound system — it was a room that would let you in dressed however you were dressed, at a point in London where a lot of rooms wouldn't.

That is the whole institution, and it explains why the crowd looks the way it does. Almost nowhere in nightlife has a continuous forty-year membership. Slimelight has people who were at the first event standing near people who were born two decades after it, and the thing they have in common isn't an era of music — it's this address.

The management changed in 2022, when Ricardo Castro took over after co-founder Mayuan Mak died. Castro's stated project has been to bring in a younger crowd without evicting the old one, and the programming reflects it: three floors, each given to a different stretch of what he calls the dark scene. Ground floor holds the foundations — 80s goth and synth pop early, EBM and industrial as it gets late. Upstairs is harder, faster and considerably more glow-in-the-dark. There are live performances now, which there were not before.

## Practical, because the listings won't tell you

The building is [seven spaces across five levels](https://www.electrowerkz.co.uk/) with a licence to 6am, and Slimelight has historically run until the tube starts. Members pay roughly half the door price; the membership system is old and a lot of the room is on it. As of the last detailed reporting, that was £20 in and £10 for members — **treat any price you read here as needing a check**, including this one.

Dressing up is not decorative here, it's the actual activity. The mirrors are three deep with people fixing backcombing, the stairwells work as photo sets, and there are usually several club photographers moving through the night. If you turn up in normal clothes nobody will throw you out, but you will have misunderstood what you came to.

## Who should go

Anyone who wants to see what a subculture looks like when it's been allowed to grow old in one place instead of being priced out of it. London has lost an enormous amount of this kind of room — the same erosion that runs under Near's piece on <NearLink slug="dalston-superstore-london">Dalston Superstore</NearLink> — and the ones that survive tend to survive because they built something with members rather than customers.

Go for the continuity and the wardrobe. Go on a night it's actually on. And if you're assembling a full weekend rather than one late one, <NearLink slug="central-station-kings-cross-london">Central Station</NearLink> is fifteen minutes west and makes a related argument about how long a room can hold onto its own people.
