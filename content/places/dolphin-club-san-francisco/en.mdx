The water in Aquatic Park cove sits at 55 to 58 degrees most of the year, which is cold enough that most people's relationship with San Francisco Bay begins and ends with looking at it from the sidewalk. The Dolphin Club exists to talk you out of that. It's a volunteer-run swimming and rowing club at 502 Jefferson Street that's been putting members in this water since 1877, and three days a week it lets anyone else in too — no membership, no sponsor, just a waiver and $12.

## What founded in 1877 actually means here

A group of German immigrants started the Dolphin Club as a small boat club at the water's edge near Bay and Leavenworth, decades before this stretch of shoreline looked anything like [Aquatic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin_Club_(San_Francisco)). It moved twice — a Van Ness boathouse in 1895, then in 1938 to its current building at 502 Jefferson, shifted along with its neighbor to make room for the Maritime Museum going up next door. The club stayed male-only until 1976, when six women filed suit, arguing the club couldn't restrict membership on land it leased from the city under federal public-access law. They won. Women were admitted in 1977 and now make up close to 40% of a membership that's grown past 2,000, a long way from the original 25.

Here's the quirk worth knowing before you show up: the Dolphin Club and the [South End Rowing Club](https://serc.com/) are two entirely separate, entirely serious volunteer clubs, sharing the same cove from adjacent buildings — 502 and 500 Jefferson. Neither is open to walk-in guests every day. Dolphin Club takes the public Monday, Wednesday and Friday; South End covers Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. Between the two, the cove is guest-accessible most days of the week, but show up on the wrong club's day and you're locked out of that one specifically — the beach itself stays public and free regardless, no club needed, if you just want to swim from the sand. [Current hours and the exact fee](https://dolphinclub.org/) are worth a check before you go: the club's own visit page lists $12 cash or check, $12.67 by card — a couple dollars past the figure that still circulates on older write-ups, which is the kind of drift that happens to a 149-year-old institution's guest pricing over time.

## Going

Bring your own suit, cap, goggles and towel — the club provides the water, the sauna, and not much else. There's no wetsuit culture here; going in bare is close to the point, and the hot sauna afterward is the actual reward structure that keeps people coming back through a San Francisco winter. If you want to see what the club actually looks like at full volume rather than day-use quiet, the [New Year's Day Alcatraz swim](https://dolphinclub.org/) is a 40-year tradition — Dolphin and South End members jumping off Alcatraz at 8am on January 1, 2027, and racing the 1.25-mile crossing back to this same cove. It's a members' race, not something you can enter as a guest, but you can watch the whole thing from the beach for free, which is a better cold-open to the club's culture than a single day-use visit gives you.
