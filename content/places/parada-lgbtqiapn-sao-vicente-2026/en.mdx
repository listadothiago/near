Mark the date before the details fill in: São Vicente's own Pride parade — its **17th edition** — is confirmed for **November 22, 2026**. That comes straight from the event's own [official Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/paradasvoficial/) (@paradasvoficial: *"SUA PARADA ACONTECE DIA 22 DE NOVEMBRO"*), matched against [Mundo GTV's statewide calendar](https://www.mundogtv.com.br/noticia/calendario-atualizado-das-paradas-lgbtqiapn-de-sp-2026-e-divulgado) of Pride parades across São Paulo state. A Diversity Fair runs the day before, November 21, per that same account. That's further out than most of Near's dated events get to run with, which is exactly the point — book around it now, rather than finding out about it the week of.

## Why this beach, specifically

The parade runs on **Praia do Itararé**, and that's not incidental. This stretch of sand has been Baixada Santista's LGBTQIA+ territory for close to forty years — long enough that <NearLink slug="quiosque-da-cris-sao-vicente">Quiosque da Cris</NearLink>, the beach kiosk that raised the area's first pride flag back in the 1980s, still anchors the same coastline the parade closes out each year. The city government running an official parade here is a relatively recent, formal recognition of a claim this community staked at a pastel cart decades earlier — the parade came after the flag, not the other way around.

That history is also what's making the beach feel different right now, ahead of the parade itself. Thiago Baraldi, Near's operator, lives in São Vicente and told us directly this week: he's seeing a real, live uptick in queer and nonbinary beachgoers on Itararé this spring, not just around a single event weekend. That's a curator's own on-the-ground read, not a press claim — see [his profile](/about/thiago-baraldi) for more on how Near sources local testimony like this.

## Who runs it, and what to expect

The parade is organized by **APOLGBT** (Associação da Parada do Orgulho LGBTQIAPN de São Vicente), with support from the city's Culture Secretariat — a genuinely public, government-backed event rather than a promoter's private party, per [São Vicente's own city hall coverage](https://www.saovicente.sp.gov.br/informacao/orgulho-a-beira-mar-parada-lgbtqia-movimenta-sao-vicente-neste-domingo-16) of a recent edition.

Recent years' editions have run a Diversity Fair and a "Diva das Divas" competition alongside the parade itself, with singers and DJs performing through the afternoon — [BS9's coverage](https://www.bs9.com.br/litoral/sao-vicente-tem-feira-da-diversidade-e-15a-parada-lgbtqiapn/28361/) of the most recent documented edition put it running roughly 1pm to 8pm, near the Ilha Porchat Clube end of the beach. Neither the exact 2026 start time nor this year's specific stage location has been published yet as of this writing — the hours above are a reasonable estimate based on the pattern, not a confirmed schedule, and worth double-checking against APOLGBT's own channels as the date gets closer.

## Beyond the one day

São Paulo's coast made [the state's official LGBTQIAPN+ tourism map](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/diario-mais/litoral-de-sp-entra-no-mapa-do-turismo-lgbtqiapn-veja-cidades/190659/) recently, which is the kind of formal recognition that tends to follow years of an actual scene existing, not the other way around. If November is too far out, the year-round version of this same territory is a short walk down the same beach at Quiosque da Cris, or a short drive to <NearLink slug="amuse-beach-club-sao-vicente">Amuse Beach Club</NearLink> on Ilha Porchat for the nightlife side of the same community.
