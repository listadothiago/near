Rodrigo Levino spent years writing for Folha de S.Paulo, Playboy and VIP before the print industry he worked in started shedding jobs and budgets around 2010. In [an August 2026 interview with Coifa](https://coifa.substack.com/p/entrevista-rodrigo-levino-fala-sobre), São Paulo's independent food-drink Substack, he put the moment plainly: "a estrutura estava ruindo, já não tinha mais grana, mais espaço" — the structure was collapsing, there was no more money, no more space. He left, and in 2016 opened **Jesuíno Brilhante** in Pinheiros instead.

## A crisis-era restaurant built to stay cheap

Levino didn't frame the restaurant as a passion project with no constraints. As he told Coifa: "vou abrir um restaurante no período de crise, então ele precisa ser acessível" — he was opening in a crisis, so it had to be accessible. That's stayed true a decade on: Google's own pricing data puts a meal at R$60-80 a person, modest for a kitchen this specific about sourcing.

The menu isn't restaurant-invented sertão cuisine — it's family recipe, transcribed. "É o cardápio da minha mãe, da minha avó, das minhas tias," Levino says: his mother's, grandmother's and aunts' cooking, built around what people in the RN backlands actually had — meat, cassava, little else — turned into carne de sol, baião de dois and cajuína. Levino now runs the kitchen with chef Kátia Mello, per [CNN Brasil's coverage](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/viagemegastronomia/gastronomia/restaurante-jesuino-brilhante-em-sp-reune-chefs-do-shihoma-e-ca-ja-para-comemoracao-de-seu-aniversario/) of the restaurant's ongoing anniversary programming.

## The supply chain is the whole point

Getting the food right meant rebuilding a supply line, not just a menu. Levino sources directly from Caicó and Patu — his own hometown region — through suppliers who'd previously worked with his father, João Batista Rodrigues, who became the restaurant's face until his death in 2020. Trucks make the roughly 3,000-kilometer run from the RN sertão to Pinheiros over three to four days, weekly, which is a genuinely unusual level of commitment for a neighborhood lunch spot rather than a tasting-menu destination.

## What's real about the reputation, and what isn't

Jesuíno Brilhante isn't a discovery — [Catraca Livre](https://catracalivre.com.br/gastronomia/jesuino-brilhante-achados-elo/) and Tripadvisor have covered it for years, and a real, dated [10th-anniversary street party ran May 31, 2026](https://vivaacidadenews.com.br/events/jesuino-brilhante-10-anos-forro-calcada/), with guest-chef dinners and a house recipe book still to come this year, no further date confirmed yet.

Google's 1,786 reviews average 4.7, with most naming carne de sol and baião de dois specifically; one recent reviewer disagreed on seasoning, calling a dish under-salted for a cuisine built on bold flavor — a fair minority complaint to weigh against the pattern. No multi-hour queue or viral moment shows up in that record; it's simply been consistently good since 2016. Google also flags the restaurant as a business that welcomes the LGBTQ+ community.

It's on Rua Arruda Alvim, 187, open for lunch Monday through Saturday, closed Sunday — closer to a neighborhood institution than a stop on a hype circuit, which is exactly the register Levino set out to build.
