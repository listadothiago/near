Most bars that call themselves "hi-fi" mean they bought good speakers. Jumbi means something more specific and more stubborn: there is exactly one turntable in the building, no mixer, no beatmatching, no second deck waiting in the wings. A DJ puts a record on, it plays, and when it ends they pick the next one — the whole architecture of the night is built around forcing people to actually listen rather than just occupy a room while music happens near them.

## The gear, because the gear is the point

The booth at the back runs two Quad 405 power amps and a Quad 606 into a pair of Tannoy Little Red Monitors — vintage British hi-fi kit, the kind of components audiophiles argue about in forums, repurposed here as a club sound system rather than a living-room setup. The turntable itself is a custom job from Isonoe London, running a Master Sounds external power supply and Grado Labs cartridges. None of it is decoration. Co-founder Bradley Zero — who also runs the Rhythm Section International label and party series — stocks the booth from his own personal vinyl collection, and visiting selectors are handed the crates rather than a laptop input. It's a deliberately inefficient way to run a bar, and that inefficiency is the entire draw for anyone who's tired of clubs where the DJ could be anyone behind a MacBook.

## A kitchen with its own reasons

Jumbi opened in July 2022, founded by Zero alongside Nathanael Williams (one half of the production duo The Colours That Rise), and the food side isn't an afterthought bolted onto a music venue — it's the other half of the same premise, built on the founders' own Caribbean heritage rather than a generic "island vibes" menu. The kitchen smokes jerk chicken on the drum for 48 hours, runs Nanny's curry goat, plates oxtail Trini doubles and plantain tostones with scotch bonnet hummus, and has hosted the Original Flava Kitchen residency doing Jamaican-Turkish fusion plates like curry goat manti. The drinks list leans hard into rum — a house rum punch, a banana mai tai, and a bottle list that specifically spotlights Black-owned rum producers rather than the usual big-distillery defaults. This is a restaurant with a standalone dinner-reservation flow, not bar snacks, which is why it's filed here under both nightlife and food.

## Finding it, and getting in

Jumbi sits at Unit 4.1, Copeland Park, 133 Copeland Road, Peckham SE15 3SN — inside the sprawling ex-industrial Copeland Park and Bussey Building complex that's become Peckham's unofficial cultural quarter, home to a rooftop cinema, studios, and a rotating cast of bars and galleries behind the same unassuming brick frontage. Jumbi is open Tuesday through Sunday (closed Monday), and entry is free most nights, with Friday and Saturday free before 10pm before a cover kicks in later. Tuesdays bring a paid Orii Jam Terrace takeover via DICE. One flag worth carrying rather than glossing over: the venue's own site states plainly that it is not currently wheelchair accessible, citing steep steps and narrow doorways at the entrance — worth knowing before you plan a visit around it.

## The one date to actually put in a calendar

Jumbi runs a packed events calendar most weeks — Friday takeovers, a daytime open-air series through the summer, one-off collectives — but the specific date worth booking around in 2026 is New Year's Eve. **Jumbi presents: A VERY SPECIAL NYE** lands on Wednesday 31 December 2026: dinner service runs 4-8pm on a reservations-only basis, then the venue flips to a ticketed dance floor from 7pm through to 4am, with a lineup including blissy e, Donut, HARUNA, Maxwell Owin, Steffi Bricks, and Steven Julien. Early-bird tickets for the night have already sold out per the listing, which tells you most of what you need to know about how this "raucous, party-driven church to music," as one write-up called it, has been building a following since 2022 without ever giving up the single-turntable premise that started it.
