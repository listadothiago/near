The quickest way to tell whether a grassroots music venue is actually healthy is not to look at the room. It is to look at how far ahead you can buy a ticket. At the Green Door Store, in the railway arches under Brighton station, you can currently buy one for [January 2028](https://dice.fm/venue/green-door-store-zg92).

## Why a booking calendar is the story

That sounds like trivia. It is not, and the reason is in [Music Venue Trust's Annual Report 2025](https://www.musicvenuetrust.com/2026/01/music-venue-trust-annual-report-2025/), published in January 2026.

Thirty grassroots music venues closed permanently over the year it covers. More than half — 53% — of the ones still open made no profit at all, on average margins of 2.5%. Six thousand jobs went, a 19% contraction of the workforce in a single year.

And the number that matters most for a room like this one: **175 UK towns and cities, home to an estimated 25 million people, no longer receive regular touring shows by professional artists.** The circuit has not just thinned. In a lot of the country it has stopped.

MVT's chief executive Mark Davyd put the arithmetic plainly at the launch: ["We have reached the limits of what venues can absorb on margins of 2.5%."](https://www.musicvenuetrust.com/2026/01/music-venue-trust-annual-report-2025/)

## What is actually on sale

Against that, here is the Green Door Store's forward book, from the venue's own ticketing: **TV Priest on Thursday 28 January 2027** (£16.23), **Rowena Wise on Saturday 13 March 2027** (£15.87), and **ATLAS on Thursday 27 January 2028** (£14.73). Between now and then it runs most nights of the week.

That is what a functioning touring stop looks like, and 175 towns in the same country do not have one.

Worth correcting one thing that circulates about this place, because it is the sort of claim Brighton listings repeat without checking. You will see it described as one of the "top 11 most iconic music venues in the country" per Music Venue Trust. That phrasing does not appear anywhere on Music Venue Trust's own site, and the venue's own Instagram bio does not use it either — it cites an NME shortlist and a 2024 Music Week grassroots nomination instead.

What *is* verifiable at source: the venue is a member of the Music Venue Alliance, and when Marshall and MVT launched **Marshall Nights** — over 60 rising artists across more than 20 grassroots venues — the series [kicked off here](https://www.nme.com/news/music/marshall-team-up-with-music-venue-trust-to-champion-60-local-artists-in-grassroots-venues-3904976), alongside Cardiff's Fuel, Glasgow's Rum Shack and Sheffield's Yellow Arch. That is a better fact than the one being repeated.

## Cobbles, and the honest version of "accessible"

The building sits in the lower goods yard and, by the venue's own account, was once a horse hospital for the railway. The floor is still the yard's.

The venue's [access page](https://thegreendoorstore.co.uk/venue-info/access/) is unusually detailed and worth reading before you go: one level throughout, small ramps and widened doorways, a roughly 40mm step on the first set of entry doors with a completely flat central set, two wheelchair spaces per show, a lowered bar, an accessible toilet on a RADAR key, gender-neutral toilets throughout, and a Personal Assistant ticket scheme. Strobe is used, and earplugs are sold at the bar. Access queries go to access@thegreendoorstore.co.uk with five working days' notice.

Now the part the access page understates. Reviewers keep raising the same physical detail: the floor is uneven. One recent Google reviewer calls it ["completely uneven"](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Green+Door+Store/@50.8295937,-0.1401994,17z) while loving the place anyway. Step-free and level is true; smooth is not. If that matters for you, it is worth knowing in advance rather than at the door.

Two other patterns run through the recent reviews, and both are about the door rather than the room. Entry can be slow and unmarshalled — one reviewer in mid-2026 described the queueing system as "abysmal", with no barriers to stop people pushing in. And accounts of door staff are genuinely mixed, in a way the warm reviews of the bar staff are not. The room has a 4.3 average across more than 900 reviews; the complaints cluster at the entrance.

## The gig that says the most about it

If you want the single thing that distinguishes this venue from a well-run room that merely books bands: it is the long-running host of **The Rock House**, the gig night from Brighton learning-disability arts charity [Carousel](https://carousel.org.uk/whats-on/the-rock-house), where some of the bands are learning disabled and some are not. Carousel ran an all-day festival here on 4 July 2026, £6.50, carers free.

A venue on 2.5% margins giving over a full day to that is a choice, not a byproduct of being old.

Along the seafront, <NearLink slug="legends-hotel-brighton">Legends</NearLink> is Brighton's other kind of late room — a Kemptown institution that sells cabaret and a basement club. This one sells the touring circuit, at £8 to £19, under a railway station, for as long as the maths holds.
