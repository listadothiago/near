You'll walk past the entrance the first time. There's no marquee, no queue, nothing that reads as "nightlife destination" from the street — just a doorway into what is, unmistakably, a parking garage. Take the stairs (or the lift, if you're carrying drinks worth protecting) to the 4th floor, and the building's whole personality flips: exposed structural columns painted over with two and a half decades of exhibitions, a terrace with a real view over Porto's rooftops, and a room that's simultaneously a restaurant, a bar, an art gallery, and — after midnight — a dance floor. This is Maus Hábitos, and the fact that it's stacked into a 1939 Art Deco car park rather than a purpose-built venue is not incidental. It's the whole point.

## A garage that outlived cars

The building itself, [Garagem Passos Manuel](https://2018.openhouseporto.com/en/places/garagem-passos-manuel/), was designed by architect Mário Abreu and opened in 1939 as a multi-storey parking structure for Porto's downtown — a small monument to the idea that the automobile was where the city's future was heading. It's still recognizable as exactly that from the street: an Art Deco facade with the clean geometric lines of the period, standing at Rua de Passos Manuel 178 in Porto's Baixa. What nobody in 1939 planned for was that, six decades later, its top floor would get colonized by a group of artists with a different idea about what a car park's fourth floor could be for.

## Bad habits, on purpose

Maus Hábitos — Portuguese for "bad habits" — started in 1998 as an artist-led experiment in what Porto's cultural infrastructure was missing: somewhere that could be a gallery on Tuesday, a concert hall on Friday, and a clubbing floor by 1am without any of those identities crowding out the others. In 2002 the people running it, Daniel Pires, Isaque Pinheiro, João Baeta, Mário Altavilla Canijo, and Manuel César Marques, formalized the project under a cultural association they named [Saco Azul](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maus_H%C3%A1bitos_(centro_cultural)) ("blue bag"), and the space has run continuously since — through the 2008 crash, through Porto's tourism boom, through a pandemic that closed plenty of venues with a fraction of Maus Hábitos's overhead. That it's still there, still doing the same multi-hat trick, is itself a kind of proof of concept.

## What's actually in the room

The layout does real work: a Concert Hall that hosts bands and then, once the last set ends, becomes the night's dance floor with bar service running straight through; a Grand Hall functioning as a restaurant with its own cocktail programming and DJ sets; the MUPI Gallery, dedicated to photography and digital art with a bar built into the same room; and a separate Exhibition Room that's carried 25-plus years of contemporary shows by working and emerging artists. Outdoors, there's an interior courtyard nicknamed the "Aquarium" and a rooftop terrace, both of which turn Maus Hábitos into one of the few spots in central Porto where you can get real air and a skyline view without leaving a venue that's also, at the same moment, running a concert downstairs. Artists whose work has moved through the space over the decades include Miguel Januário (better known by his tag +Maismenos), Rute Rosas, Pedro Maia, and Miguel Duarte — not a roster of tourist-circuit names, but the kind of list that means something if you actually follow Porto's contemporary art scene rather than skimming a guidebook.

## Not a museum piece

The easy failure mode for a 25-year-old alternative space is to calcify into a nostalgia act — the place everyone agrees was important once. Maus Hábitos has mostly dodged that by staying genuinely multi-purpose rather than settling into one identity: it still runs a kitchen daily, still rotates gallery shows monthly, still books bands and DJs, and it expanded the concept into new cities (Lisbon and Vila Real, both opened in 2021) rather than resting on the Porto original. That expansion is worth knowing about if you're chasing the brand elsewhere in Portugal, but the fourth floor on Rua de Passos Manuel is still the one with the weight of 25 years and an actual garage underneath it — the version worth the climb.

If Maus Hábitos is your entry point into Porto's less-touristed cultural scene, it pairs naturally with <NearLink slug="gato-vadio-porto">Gato Vadio</NearLink>, the independent bookshop a short walk away in Cedofeita that carries a lot of the same do-it-yourself, artist-run energy in daylight hours that Maus Hábitos carries after dark. Go to one for the books and the talks, the other for the version of the same instinct that ends in a dance floor.

## Practical notes

Maus Hábitos is at Rua de Passos Manuel 178, 4th floor, 4000-382 Porto — look for the Garagem Passos Manuel building itself rather than a separate storefront, since the venue's entrance is the garage's own stairwell/lift. The kitchen runs daily until around 11pm (later on weekends), with the bar, gallery rooms, and clubbing floor keeping later hours than the restaurant. Given how much the venue's own event listings churn month to month — gallery shows rotate monthly, and the concert/DJ calendar changes weekly — check [the official site](https://www.maushabitos.com/en/) or its Instagram (@maushabitos) close to your visit date rather than relying on any specific lineup; what doesn't change is the building, the layout, and the fact that whatever's on, it's rarely just one thing at a time.
