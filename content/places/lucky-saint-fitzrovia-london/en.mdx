Most pubs that stock a non-alcoholic beer treat it as a single line at the bottom of the tap list, there for the one friend who's driving. [The Lucky Saint](https://luckysaint.co/pages/the-lucky-saint-pub-london-w1) inverts that: it's a pub built by an alcohol-free beer brand, opened in 2023 on Devonshire Street in Fitzrovia, where the 0.5% lager sits on the bar next to Guinness and cask ale as an equally serious option rather than the compromise pick.

## Not a dry bar — a pub that stopped pretending alcohol-free is the exception

This isn't a sober bar in the Ocean Beach Café or Sipeos sense of a menu built entirely without alcohol. The Lucky Saint pours real cask ale and keg Guinness alongside its own 0.5% beer, and [CAMRA's current listing](https://camra.org.uk/pubs/lucky-saint-london-129248) confirms it's operating as a genuine pub, not a product showroom. What's different is which drink gets treated as the default. A room where the alcohol-free option is engineered by the same brewers with the same care as everything else on tap reads differently to someone who's sober-curious than a room where it's a grudging concession — the difference between being offered water and being offered a drink that happens to have no alcohol in it.

The ground floor is the pub itself: bar snacks, small plates, a cheeseboard, dog-friendly seating. Upstairs is Lucky Saint's own office, event space and podcast studio, which tells you this is a brand headquarters as much as a boozer — worth knowing if you're after a purely anonymous pint rather than a slightly branded evening.

## Why bother

Fitzrovia has plenty of pubs where "I'm not drinking tonight" gets a slightly apologetic half-pint of Coke. The Lucky Saint is one of the few places in central London where that sentence doesn't need an explanation, because the whole business exists to make the alcohol-free choice unremarkable rather than notable.
