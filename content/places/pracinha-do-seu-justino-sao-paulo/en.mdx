Two bars share a name and a street in Vila Madalena, and untangling them is worth doing before you show up at the wrong door. [Seu Justino](https://www.instagram.com/seujustino/) is the original — Rua Harmonia 77, live music, a Sunday feijoada, the kind of rustic-eclectic room that built a following past 120,000 followers on Instagram. Pracinha do Seu Justino is the same operator's [open-air spinoff](https://www.guiadasemana.com.br/bares-drinks/noticia/pracinha-do-seu-justino-inaugura-na-vila-madalena) a few doors down at number 117, built as its own space rather than an extension of the first. They're related, not identical, and both are still running — which matters, because plenty of aggregator listings conflate the two addresses into one entry.

What Pracinha adds is the format: an open-air praça (little square, hence the name) built around boteco-style tables, vertical gardens climbing the walls, and neon signage doing the mood lighting. Guia da Semana's coverage frames it as inspired by São Paulo street food, and the menu leans that way — shareable plates, tapioca, barbecue ribs — rather than trying to replicate the original's sit-down feijoada program. The drinks side runs to tropical cocktails and a standing Thursday promotion pouring Colorado draft, which is the kind of recurring hook that turns a Tuesday into a destination night rather than a fallback. [BaresSP's listing](https://www.baressp.com.br/bares/pracinha-do-seu-justino) also flags it as a dog-friendly room — worth knowing if the open-air layout is the actual draw and you'd rather not leave the dog at home for it.

Hours are the other thing worth getting right, since the two venues don't run the same schedule. Pracinha opens Tuesday through Friday from 6pm and Saturday and Sunday from noon — an early weekend start that makes it workable for a long lazy lunch as well as a night out, unlike a straight nightlife room that doesn't open its doors before dark. Reachable by the same walk that takes in <NearLink slug="beco-do-batman-sao-paulo">Beco do Batman</NearLink>, the street-art alley a few blocks over — Vila Madalena rewards treating an afternoon there as a loop rather than a single stop.

The honest caveat: names change hands and gastro-bar concepts get refreshed on a shorter cycle than a neighborhood restaurant, so don't take the current décor or exact menu as fixed in stone. What's confirmed as of this writing is that both addresses are open, both belong to the same team, and the split between them — one for the sit-down original, one for the open-air street-food room — is a real one, not a rebrand that left a stale duplicate online.
