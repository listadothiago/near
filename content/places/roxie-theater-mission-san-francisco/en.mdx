On 16th Street in the Mission, the [Roxie](https://roxie.com/) has been running films longer than any other cinema in San Francisco — a claim [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxie_Theater) backs with a history stretching back to 1909, making it a genuine survivor of a century that closed most of its peers.

## A nonprofit trying to finally own its home

The Roxie operates as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, not a commercial chain, which shapes what it programs: repertory series, cult double-bills like *D.E.B.S.*, and themed runs such as Bleak Week and Floating Features that a profit-driven multiplex has no reason to book. [Screen Slate's listings](https://www.screenslate.com/venues/roxie-theater) show programming scheduled well into December 2026.

After more than a century renting its own building, the theater has launched a fundraising campaign to actually buy it — [reported by the SF Examiner](https://www.sfexaminer.com/) — a rare shot at converting a century of tenancy into ownership, and one worth knowing about for anyone who wants an independent cinema like this to still exist in another hundred years.
