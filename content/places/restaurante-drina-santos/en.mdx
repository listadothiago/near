Rua Oswaldo Cochrane in Embaré has had the same buffet running off it since 1967 — [Restaurante Drina](https://www.instagram.com/drina_restaurante/), a pay-by-weight house that built its menu around a vegetable garden it still tends itself rather than a supplier's delivery crates. Fifty-nine years on the same street is the kind of number restaurants tend to round up or invent; this one's confirmed by the house's own account and cross-checked against [Tripadvisor's listing](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g303625-d12854386-Reviews-Restaurante_Recanto_Drina-Santos_State_of_Sao_Paulo.html), which puts it at 4.6 out of 5 across a real spread of reviews rather than a handful of five-star plants.

## What's actually on the buffet

The daily spread is straightforward home cooking — rice, beans, stews, salads pulled from that same garden — but the calendar has real structure to it rather than sameness every day. Friday and Sunday bring Arabic specialties; Wednesday and Saturday the buffet turns to feijoada. Roasted goat and apple strudel show up as the two dishes regulars mention by name, an unusual pairing that reads less like a gimmick and more like a kitchen that's kept whatever worked across nearly six decades rather than chasing a single cuisine.

It's a pay-by-weight format, so there's no set menu to overthink — you fill a plate, it gets weighed, you sit down. That structure rewards a light first pass and a second lap once you've seen what's actually out, which regulars clearly already know.

## The garden and the yard

The vegetable garden is the detail that separates this from a generic per-kilo buffet: it's not a marketing line, it's where a meaningful share of what's on the counter actually comes from, per the house's own posts on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/drina_restaurante/). The outdoor seating area, which the restaurant calls "Nosso Quintal" ("our backyard"), extends that same register — pet-friendly, open-air, closer to eating at a relative's house than a restaurant.

## Where it sits in Santos

Santos already has a food-drink register for a tasting menu (<NearLink slug="made-cozinha-autoral-santos">Made Cozinha Autoral</NearLink>) and one for a standing pastel counter (<NearLink slug="cafe-carioca-santos">Café Carioca</NearLink>). Drina is the third register neither of those covers — a sit-down, pay-by-weight house built for a regular weekday lunch rather than an occasion. Embaré itself sits a short ride from the beachfront strip, closer to where Santos residents actually eat than where visitors are steered by default.

## Before you go

Drina is open Tuesday through Sunday, 11:30 AM to 3:00 PM (to 3:30 PM on weekends) — a lunch-only window with no dinner service, so plan the visit accordingly. Confirm current hours before heading over, since a house running the same format for this long tends to keep its own rhythm rather than a chain's posted schedule.
