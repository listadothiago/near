A steakhouse with a bone-marrow nihari gravy on the menu isn't hedging between two cuisines — it's [combining a British chophouse's cuts with an Indian kitchen's sauces](https://www.timeout.com/newyork/news/this-new-british-indian-chophouse-is-the-latest-in-michelin-chef-sujan-sarkars-big-hudson-yards-takeover-080426) on purpose. That's Elder, which opened August 6, 2026, on the ground floor of Henry Hall at 517 W. 38th St. in Hudson Yards, from chef Sujan Sarkar, whose Chicago restaurant Indienne carries a Michelin star.

The chops carry the room: Berkshire pork rib chop vindaloo masala, burrah-spice lamb chops, a Tellicherry peppercorn-crusted filet mignon, [each finished with a sauce built like a curry rather than a reduction](https://cititour.com/NYC_News/Chef-Sujan-Sarkar-Opens-Elder-a-British-Indian-Steakhouse-in-NYC/10918) — bone-marrow nihari gravy, red pepper makhani, a green sauce that reads more chutney than herb butter.

## Part of a bigger bet on one building

Elder is the second of three rooms Sarkar is opening inside Henry Hall — Indienne, his fine-dining transplant, opened in May, and a cocktail bar called Apas is still to come by year's end. That's a single chef staking a real claim on one Hudson Yards address rather than one restaurant testing the neighborhood, closer in spirit to how <NearLink slug="rabbit-books-and-bar-east-village-nyc">a single owner building two identities into one room</NearLink> works when the bet is on a place, not just a menu. Early reviews (4.7 stars, still a small sample) single out the lamb chops specifically — worth asking for if the table's sharing.
