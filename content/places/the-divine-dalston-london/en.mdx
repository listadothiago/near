The Glory closed in Haggerston; [Jonny Woo, John Sizzle and Colin Rothbart](https://thedivine.co.uk/about/) reopened a short walk away in Dalston as The Divine, and — unusually for a scene where a closure usually just means a closure — brought the actual competitions with them. [LIPSYNC1000 and MAN UP](https://www.attitude.co.uk/culture/scene/the-divine-announcer-457055/), the lip-sync and drag-king contests The Glory built its reputation on, run here now, continuous rather than revived.

[Attitude](https://www.attitude.co.uk/culture/scene/the-divine-announcer-457055/) called it The Glory's "naughty little sister," which undersells the scale: 200 capacity across two floors, upgraded stage and lighting, and a booking calendar that runs cabaret, club nights, comedy heats and drag-king battles most nights of the week rather than saving the good stuff for Friday and Saturday. [The venue's own listings](https://thedivine.co.uk/events/) show that density directly if you want to time a visit around something specific.

The room itself splits opinion. One Google reviewer with the former Glory as a direct point of comparison called The Divine "a bit more sterile... and much less character, but very friendly staff." Others flag the practical friction of a busy queer venue: a queue to reach the smoking area, no re-entry stamp, water hard to get to on a packed dance floor. None of it reads as a reason to skip the room — the friendliness of the staff comes up unprompted in more than one account — but go in expecting a working club on a full night, not a curated lounge.

For the rest of Dalston's queer cluster — locals still call this stretch the "pink triangle" — see <NearLink slug="dalston-superstore-london">Dalston Superstore</NearLink>, a few doors down and open considerably earlier in the evening.
