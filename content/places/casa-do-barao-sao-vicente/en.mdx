**Casa do Barão** sits at Rua Frei Gaspar, 280, four blocks from the beach in downtown São Vicente — the town Martim Afonso de Sousa founded in 1532, Brazil's oldest permanent settlement. Everywhere else Near has covered here sits on the water: [AMUSE Beach Club](/pins/amuse-beach-club-sao-vicente), [Quiosque da Cris](/pins/quiosque-da-cris-sao-vicente), the [Parada LGBTQIAPN+](/pins/parada-lgbtqiapn-sao-vicente-2026) — all Ilha Porchat and Itararé.

This is the old town instead, not trying to be a beach resort.

## What was here before the house

The mansion dates to around 1865, built for landowner Coronel José Lopes. It became "Casa do Barão" after 1921, when Lopes's son-in-law — coffee trader Gustav Kurt von Pritzelwitz, called "the Baron" though untitled, per [Diário do Litoral](https://www.diariodolitoral.com.br/sao-vicente/casa-do-barao-em-sao-vicente-pode-ser-tombada-pelo-iphan/152054/) — rebuilt it. The nickname outlasted the family: by 1959 it passed to the federal government, which still owns it.

## Three archives under one roof

The house is now the [Instituto Histórico e Geográfico de São Vicente](https://casadobarao.org.br/), stacking a lot into 1,600 square meters: the Historical and General Museum (around 26,000 items — taxidermied fauna, 1932 Revolution memorabilia, the baron's own furniture), the Memorial da História Vicentina, and the Memorial Padre José de Anchieta, honoring the Jesuit who helped found the town. Its library holds 36,000-plus volumes, oldest dated 1753.

City, state and municipal councils have heritage-protected the building; a 2021 push petitioned IPHAN for national status too, unconfirmed as finalized.

## The garden, and one thing Near couldn't confirm

The 6,500-square-meter grounds are genuinely wooded — [Condephaat's own listing](https://www.ipatrimonio.org/sao-vicente-casa-do-barao-e-cobertura-vegetal/) protects the vegetation, describing centuries-old native trees as an urban bird refuge.

Near looked specifically for reports of monkeys in that garden — a detail that circulates locally. Nothing in the institute's materials, the heritage listing, regional press, or 22 recent Google reviews mentions them; the search instead surfaced São Vicente's separate Parque Ecológico Voturuá, a municipal zoo-park elsewhere in the city, likely the source of any confusion. Treat it as unverified.

## Visiting

The institute runs Monday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–6 p.m.; the museum keeps shorter hours inside that window, 2–5 p.m., per [the institute's site](https://casadobarao.org.br/) and the [city's tourism page](https://www.saovicente.sp.gov.br/visite-sao-vicente-1/o-que-fazer/casa-do-barao). Entry runs on volunteer guides, well-reviewed on Google, plus a small café, Café do Barão, and adult courses ("professor Miro"'s singing class comes up more than once).

In August 2026 the house hosted the third Festival Indígena de São Vicente with the Tekoa Paranapuã Indigenous Community — check the institute's channels for a future edition rather than assume a fixed date.
