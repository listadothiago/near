Holy Crust runs out of a rustic, foodtruck-style setup on Rua 15 de Novembro in Parque Santa Mônica — order at the counter, no cutlery on offer, no ice for drinks, and your pizza gets called out when it's ready rather than delivered table-side. It's a deliberate choice, not a budget shortcut: the dough itself is made with 5stagioni flour and a slow fermentation, the combination that gives real Neapolitan pizza its inflated, leoparded crust rather than a flat, evenly browned one.

## The pizza is doing the talking

One Google reviewer who's eaten pizza in Italy called Holy Crust's version the best they'd had, full stop — pepperoni gets singled out repeatedly across reviews as the standout, alongside praise for a thin, sauce-forward base that doesn't lean on toppings to cover for the crust. Pizzas come cut into six pieces, sized for two people to share comfortably.

![A charred, wood-fired pepperoni pizza with fresh basil at Holy Crust, São Carlos](https://near.tips/places/holy-crust-sao-carlos/hero.jpg)

Budget-wise, Google's own diner-reported range is R$40-60 per person, based on 38 people who logged what they spent — reviewers call it fair for the quality rather than cheap, which tracks with a kitchen built around a slower, more deliberate dough process than a standard flatbread operation.

## Worth calling ahead before you go

One thing worth flagging plainly: Google Maps currently lists Holy Crust as temporarily closed, with no hours shown. That reads as inconsistent with the rest of the record — the official Instagram's bio still lists standing Wednesday-through-Sunday, 6pm-11pm hours, and comments as recent as May 2026 include diners calling it the best pizza in São Carlos, which points to either a short pause or a stale Google flag rather than a real closure.
