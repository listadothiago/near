Most "day café, night bar" concepts pick a lane and half-commit to the other one — a coffee shop that also, technically, sells wine, or a bar that has a pour-over machine nobody uses after 10am. Stray Dog is trying something more specific: a genuine Shanghai all-day café model, where the same room and the same kitchen are meant to feel equally correct at 8am and 11pm, not like two businesses sharing a lease.

The morning-through-afternoon side runs on Ritual Coffee Roasters beans, which already tells you something about where the bar is set — Ritual is a serious, well-regarded San Francisco roaster, not a house-brand shortcut. From there the menu leans into flavors that read as genuinely Shanghai-café rather than pan-Asian-fusion-as-marketing-copy: pandan, ube, and a lot of citrus threaded through the espresso drinks, including a coconut water topped with espresso foam that's the kind of drink you either specifically go looking for or have never heard of. That specificity is the whole bet the café is making — it's not trying to be an approachable, familiar coffee shop with a few "fun" drinks bolted on.

At night, the identity flips without the room changing. Bar partner Nathan Angelo Depante runs a list of 12 to 14 pan-Asian cocktails, a genuinely large rotating menu for a space this size, which suggests the bar program is meant to carry real weight rather than exist as an afterthought to the food-and-coffee side. Owner Angela Cao isn't new to this — Stray Dog is her second bar after Lost Cat Bar & Bites near Union Square, and the name is a direct, not-especially-subtle callback to that first project, which is the kind of detail that reads as a real throughline rather than a coincidence once you know it.

The location matters too. Stray Dog took over the space that used to house Junior, a cocktail bar, at 2545 24th Street — right in the stretch of the Mission that's having a genuinely busy 2026. Two blocks away, Cinderella Bakery & Cafe just opened its long-delayed second location after nearly eight years of permitting fights and neighborhood pushback over the site's history as La Victoria Bakery. Stray Dog isn't part of that story, but it's opening into the same block at the same moment, which makes this stretch of 24th Street worth a slower walk than usual if you're already in the neighborhood — not a single-destination trip, but a corridor with more than one reason to stop.

What's genuinely useful to know before going: this is a new opening as of July 2026, which means the kind of thing that's still settling — hours, exact seasonal drink rotation, how busy the night side gets on a given evening — is more likely to shift here than at an established spot. Go in expecting a place still finding its rhythm, and treat the specific drink names in this piece as a snapshot of the opening menu rather than a permanent lineup.

If you're mapping out a Mission afternoon, Stray Dog works well as a midpoint stop. This stretch of the Inner Mission sits within easy walking distance of Balmy Alley and Clarion Alley, two of the neighborhood's best-known mural corridors and part of a decades-old Mission tradition of public, community-painted street art — worth building an actual walking loop around rather than a single coffee stop. Start with an espresso here, spend twenty minutes actually looking at the alleys instead of just photographing them, and circle back after dark if the cocktail side is what drew you here in the first place. The Mission's 24th Street corridor rewards that kind of slower, on-foot pace more than a single-destination visit does — Stray Dog is a good anchor point for it, not the whole trip.
