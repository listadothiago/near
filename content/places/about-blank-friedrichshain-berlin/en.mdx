Walk past Ostkreuz station toward the S-Bahn tracks and you'll find ://about blank tucked into a repurposed industrial building on Markgrafendamm — no obvious signage, no velvet-rope theater, just a door that opens around 1am and a queue that tells you you're in the right place. It's been running since 2010, and it's kept the raw, unfinished-warehouse feel that a lot of Berlin's more polished clubs have long since renovated away.

## What's inside, and what's outside

Two smallish, punchy-sound dance floors carry the indoor programming — house and techno as the default, with a booking policy Resident Advisor and Time Out Berlin both describe as collective-run and left-leaning, tending toward harder, niche selections over crowd-pleasers. The real differentiator is outside: an expansive garden with a chill-out caravan and wooded corners, where DJs play in summer and — per multiple current reviews — the garden stays open through winter with a bonfire going. That's not a small detail in a city where most clubs shut their outdoor space the moment it gets cold.

## The door and the queue, honestly

Current Tripadvisor reviews are genuinely split, and worth reading both sides of rather than averaging into a star rating. One recent reviewer calls it "one of the best underground clubs in Berlin," praising the inclusive, safe atmosphere; another, just as recent, describes rude treatment at the door and an arbitrary-feeling entry policy. What's consistent across almost every account: the club doesn't open until roughly 1am, and the queue builds fast after midnight — one reviewer reported people still lined up at 5:30am. If you're planning a night here, plan for the wait, and know the door staff's reputation is a real mixed bag rather than uniformly one thing or the other.

## Worth knowing before you go

There's no dress code beyond "don't try to look like you're trying," and locals will tell you the DJ lineup matters more here than at almost any other Berlin club — check who's playing before you commit to the queue, since the crowd and the night's mood shift entirely with the booking. Friedrichshain's club cluster around Ostkreuz doesn't have the tourist-trail visibility of Kreuzberg's queer-bar corridor further west, which is part of why regulars still treat this one as a local's club rather than a stop on somebody's Berlin bucket list.
