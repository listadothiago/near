The most useful thing about this building is a constraint, so I'll lead with it instead of burying it under the architecture: **the Gilbert & George Centre is open Thursday to Sunday, 11:00 to 17:45, and closed Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday.** Turn up on a Tuesday and you get a door. That is not a footnote on a page about a free gallery — for four days out of seven it's the only fact that matters.

Two corrections while I'm here, both from [the Centre's own visitor page](https://gilbertandgeorgecentre.org/about/your-visit/) against what aggregators are publishing. Secondary listings say 10am–5pm. The Centre says 11:00–17:45. That is an hour you don't have at the start and forty-five minutes you didn't know you had at the end, and if you've built a day around the earlier number you've built it wrong.

## What the artists actually made here

Gilbert & George have worked out of Spitalfields since the 1960s, and the Centre is what happens when two artists decide to house their own legacy rather than leave it to an institution. They set up the charity in 2017, having bought the building in 2015, and opened to the public on 1 April 2023 with *THE PARADISICAL PICTURES*. SIRS Architects did the conversion with the artists: 280 square metres of exhibition space across three levels. The inaugural year drew close to 40,000 people.

What it was before is the detail worth holding onto — a former brewery complex, converted. Note where that puts it. This is a brewery reworked into a gallery, sitting a few minutes from a much larger brewery reworked into a creative quarter. The East End keeps solving the same problem with the same buildings, at different scales.

The point of the place is stated plainly and structurally: **free entry, always, on the artists' own "Art for All" principle.** That phrase does real work. A private foundation funded by two living artists could have run on timed tickets and a members' preview economy. It doesn't. You walk in off Heneage Street, and nobody asks you for anything — the shop and a suggested £5 donation exist, but neither is a gate.

The Saturday tour is the other half of that principle made operational. [A free highlight tour runs every Saturday at 4PM](https://gilbertandgeorgecentre.org/about/your-visit/), no booking, all welcome. If you have one slot to spend here and any flexibility about which day, spend it on a Saturday afternoon — a free tour of work by artists who are still alive and still around the corner is a genuinely uncommon proposition, and the exhibition reads differently once someone walks you through the logic of it.

## What's on: a memorial, and a 564-part work

The current show is *Our George Crompton, WORLDS and WINDOWS by Gilbert & George* — a tribute to their close friend George Crompton, who died in September 2025. It centres the triptych *CROMPTON STREET*, shown publicly for the first time, alongside *NUMBER TWELVE*. Around it sit two bodies of Postcard Art: the *WORLDS AND WINDOWS* group from 1989, which hasn't been exhibited since 1990, and *THE URETHRA POSTCARD ART* from 2009, a work made of 564 individual pieces.

That last number is the reason to give the room actual time rather than a lap. A 564-part work is not something you take in from the doorway; the structure only resolves once you're close enough to read the repetition. Programming turns over on its own schedule, so check [the What's On page](https://gilbertandgeorgecentre.org/whats-on/) before you travel — that's the only honest way to send anyone to a gallery.

## One dated thing, and it closes this weekend

**The Backyard Biennial: East runs 15 July to 6 September 2026 — free, eight weeks, more than forty East London partners, led by the Whitechapel Gallery.** The Gilbert & George Centre is one of them. As this publishes, that leaves exactly one open window: Thursday 3rd through Sunday 6th September, which happens to be precisely the four days the Centre is open anyway. After the 6th the biennial is over and the Centre carries on as normal — still free, still Thursday to Sunday.

## The walk this belongs to

Heneage Street is a short turning just off Brick Lane, and Aldgate East is five minutes away on the District and Hammersmith & City lines. That siting is the practical argument for coming: the <NearLink slug="old-truman-brewery-london">Old Truman Brewery</NearLink> — the other converted brewery, the one that swallowed a whole block — is a few minutes up the same road. Which makes this the natural first stop on a west-to-east East End day rather than a special trip.

Inside, photography is permitted — but no flash, no filming your visit, no tripods, no selfie sticks and no large cameras. Sharing personal shots is fine. There's no café on site, which is another argument for pairing it with Brick Lane rather than treating it as a destination on its own. Accessibility is arranged by contacting the Centre ahead on 020 7477 2484.

Go Thursday to Sunday. Go Saturday if you can. It costs nothing, and the only way to get it wrong is to arrive on the wrong day.
