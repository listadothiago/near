Uchi started as an evening izakaya in Lower Clapton, then turned its own dining room into a Japanese bakery by day — [Uchi Bake](https://londontheinside.com/location/uchi-bake/), tamago and teriyaki sandos on pillowy shokupan, matcha cookies, hojicha cheesecake. In July 2026 that daytime concept got its own address: [Uchi Bake Angel opened on Camden Passage](https://www.hot-dinners.com/2026072215242/Gastroblog/Latest-news/uchi-bake-japanese-bakery-camden-passage-angel-islington), the cobbled pedestrian row in Islington already carrying Katsute 100 and a handful of other small Japanese spots.

The Angel shop keeps the Clapton format: a day-bakery counter rather than a restaurant, doing coffee, matcha and hojicha alongside the sandos. [Hot Dinners](https://www.hot-dinners.com/2026072215242/Gastroblog/Latest-news/uchi-bake-japanese-bakery-camden-passage-angel-islington) lists beef curry buns, egg salad sando, black sesame lattes, matcha mochi cookies and hojicha cheesecake on the opening menu — the same lineup [JULIAXEATS](https://juliaxeats.com/uchi-bake-japanese-sandos-desserts-in-east-london/) prices at Clapton between £3.20 and £6.50 a item, cheap enough to order two things and split them.

## What's actually good here

The tamago sando is the thing to order first: soy-marinated whole egg on shokupan bread that [one reviewer](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Uchi+Bake+Angel/@51.534691,-0.1040013,17z) on Google Maps called "soft like a cloud," with enough filling that the sandwich holds its shape one-handed. The katsu version is the heartier option if egg salad isn't going to be lunch on its own.

On the drinks side, the hojicha latte is the house move over the matcha — roastier, less bitter if you're not used to ceremonial-grade matcha's grassiness. One recent Google reviewer flagged the drinks as "on the smaller side" relative to the food portions, which tracks with a bakery-first menu rather than a café built around large-format coffees.

Uchi Bake Angel is currently sitting at 3.7 stars across 42 Google reviews — solidly positive but not the runaway 4.8 some of London's newer bakery openings post in their first weeks, and the spread is genuine: strong marks for the sandos and atmosphere, softer notes on drink size and on a shop this new still finding its rhythm. That's a fair read for a seven-week-old second site, not a red flag.

## Getting a seat, and when to go

This isn't the queue-round-the-block story some London bakery openings become. Weekday mornings are straightforward — walk in, order, sit. Saturdays draw more of a crowd on Camden Passage generally (it's a market street), and Uchi Bake Angel gets a line at peak brunch hours, but it's the kind of line that keeps moving rather than a settle-in-for-an-hour wait. If you want the quietest version of the shop, a weekday before noon is the move; if you're building a Camden Passage morning around the market stalls, Saturday still works, just budget ten or fifteen minutes.

Camden Passage itself is worth the wider wander — vintage stalls, a small print shop, [Katsute 100](https://www.hot-dinners.com/2026072215242/Gastroblog/Latest-news/uchi-bake-japanese-bakery-camden-passage-angel-islington)'s tea ceremony room a few doors down.
