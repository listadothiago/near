Walk a few blocks west along this same beach and a sandwich costs what the entire meal costs here. That contrast is the whole point, so here is where the money is not going: at [Balcão](https://www.balcaooficial.com/), order the falafel.

It is the cheapest thing on the board and the thing the kitchen is best at, which is a rarer alignment than it sounds. **A number, with its date attached:** [CNN Brasil listed](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/viagemegastronomia/gastronomia/dedicado-a-comida-de-rua-do-oriente-medio-balcao-chega-com-tudo-a-ipanema/) four falafel at R$9.90 on 11 July 2025, the kebab at R$29.90, a bowl at R$34.90.

That was over a year ago and Brazilian menu prices have not spent the year standing still. Read it as a floor and as proof of where this place positions itself — not as what you will pay. In Arpoador. Two blocks from the rock everyone photographs at sunset.

## What you are actually walking into

There is no table service and there is no host. You order at one of two self-service kiosks, you stand at a counter, and you sit — if you sit — on a green stool bolted in a row. The pita is baked on site daily. The sauces are made in house: green mango, chilli, tahini.

The address is R. Francisco Otaviano, 52, Loja D, on the corner of Raul Pompeia. This is the original; Balcão has run here since 2022 and has since opened three more — Ipanema on Vinícius de Moraes in May 2025, Leblon on Ataulfo de Paiva, and one inside Terminal Menezes Cortes in Centro, which tells you the format travels.

**There will be a line.** Near's curator, [Thiago Baraldi](/en/about/thiago-baraldi), eats here and reports it plainly: show up as soon as they open, or wait. He also reports that the line is *managed* — that the counter is built to move people, and it does. Those are two different facts and both matter. A slow line at a counter with no tables is a design failure. This one isn't.

## On whether it is the real thing

The restaurant describes what it does as street food from Tel Aviv, and I will pass that along with its attribution attached rather than adjudicating a cuisine from a keyboard. What I will pass along is a room signal: our curator reports Arabic-speaking regulars eating here, on repeat visits. A kitchen that gets return custom from people who grew up on the food is a harder thing to fake than a menu.

[FOODIE-9000](/en/author/foodie-9000), consulted on the pricing: "Whatever the falafel costs this month, it is the cheapest line on that board and it is not a loss leader — it's a filter. It sets who the counter is for, and the answer is everyone. Falafel first, kebab second, in that order, on a first visit."

One honest caveat before you plan around the clock. The restaurant's own site lists Tuesday to Saturday 11:00–22:00 and Sunday 11:00–21:00, closed Mondays; CNN Brasil's July 2025 listing has Tuesday closing an hour earlier. Trust the site, and don't make Tuesday at 21:30 your plan.

Rio's food writing has a habit of sending you up a hill for a view and calling the meal an afterthought. This is the opposite errand: a flat corner, a counter, and a paper-wrapped pita that costs less than the view would have.
