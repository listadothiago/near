I will say something nice about a city, once, and then we can all move on.

Brockwell Lido is fifty metres of cold outdoor water in the middle of south London, and it is the only argument I have ever accepted for staying in one. [It opened on 10 July 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockwell_Lido), replacing the natural bathing ponds in Brockwell Park, and it is Grade II listed — flat-roofed, low, moderne, a boundary wall and pavilion buildings wrapped around a rectangle of water. It is not a leisure centre that happens to contain a pool. It is a piece of 1930s civic ambition that has survived long enough to become slightly improbable.

It hosted the London Swimming Championships from 1947 to 1968. Then, like most things built by councils with ambition, it was allowed to fail. **It closed in 1990.** It reopened in 1994 only because local people campaigned for it, which is the single most important fact on this page and the reason I would rather you went here than to something newer with better changing rooms.

## What it's actually like

Fifty by twenty-seven metres, outdoors, unheated, [open all year](https://historicpools.org.uk/member_pools/brockwell-lido-brixton-london/). That last clause is doing enormous work.

Year-round means January. It means getting into water that is somewhere around four or five degrees while fully aware that you have chosen this. The regulars who do that are a specific and slightly evangelical population, and they are correct — but do not let anyone tell you it is pleasant the first time. It is not. It becomes pleasant somewhere around the fourth time, which is a different claim entirely and one most write-ups skip.

In summer it is the opposite problem: it is one of the best places in London and everybody knows, so a hot Saturday means queues and a full sun terrace. Weekday mornings are the version worth planning for. There is also a gym, a studio and a hydrotherapy pool on site, which I mention for completeness rather than enthusiasm — you are here for the outdoor rectangle.

## The bit you should know before going

The lido's management changed in 2026. Fusion Lifestyle, which had run it, went into administration on 1 April, and Lambeth Council took over operations on 1 July under Active Lambeth. In the middle of that, [swimmers called a public meeting](https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2026/03/community-meeting-to-discuss-brockwell-lidos-future-as-users-seek-clarity-over-long-term-management/) to ask for clarity about the long-term arrangement.

Nothing about that means it is closing. It means the operating model is in flux, and a pool whose entire modern existence dates from a community campaign in the early nineties has users who have learned to pay attention. **Check current opening hours and prices before you travel** rather than trusting any listing, this one included — that's ordinary good practice for a lido and specifically warranted this year.

There is a wider pattern here worth naming, because it is not only about one pool. Britain built hundreds of these things between the wars, on the assumption that ordinary people were owed somewhere decent to swim outdoors, and then spent the second half of the century closing most of them. The ones still standing are almost all standing because somebody organised. That is not nostalgia, it is a live fact about how public things survive, and it is visible from the sun terrace.

## Why bother

Because outdoor swimming in a city is not a compromised version of outdoor swimming. It is its own thing: a cold, bright, slightly absurd rectangle of sky-reflecting water with a park around it and traffic somewhere beyond that. And because this particular rectangle only still exists because a neighbourhood refused to let it be filled in.

Brockwell Park is worth the walk on its own afterwards. If you want the rest of south London around it, that is genuinely a different day out from the east-London itinerary that <NearLink slug="dalston-superstore-london">Dalston Superstore</NearLink> anchors — and the two together are a decent argument that London is not one city.
