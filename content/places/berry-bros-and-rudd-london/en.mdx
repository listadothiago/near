The shop floor at 3 St James's Street slopes. Not dramatically, but enough to notice, because the building was already standing when the business moved in, and 1698 predates anyone bothering to level a floor for a shop that would still be there centuries later. [Berry Bros. & Rudd](https://www.bbr.com/) has traded wine and spirits from this address continuously since then, through the same narrow storefront wedged between St James's Palace and Pall Mall, close enough to the Palace that royal warrants aren't a marketing flourish here — they're a fact of the postcode.

What makes it worth a stop rather than a plaque to glance at is that none of this has calcified into a museum. It's still family-owned, which after 327-odd years of a trade that has swallowed most of its independent names into drinks conglomerates is the unusual part, not the age. And the business runs three Masters of Wine on staff — a qualification with a pass rate low enough that most merchants, including large ones, don't have a single one, let alone three under one roof advising on what's actually in the cellar downstairs.

The other detail worth knowing before walking in: Berry Bros. & Rudd became a certified B Corp in 2025. That's a genuinely strange pairing on paper — a three-century-old wine merchant to the Crown submitting to a modern sustainability and governance audit alongside co-ops and startups — and it's the reason this earns a spot in coverage that's otherwise allergic to anything that smells like heritage marketing. A shop this old doesn't need to prove it's still relevant; choosing to get independently audited on how it's actually run suggests it isn't coasting on the address.

None of that requires spending seriously to experience. The shop floor is open to browse, the staff are used to questions from people who aren't buying a case of first-growth Bordeaux, and the honest way in is the front door on St James's Street rather than an appointment — the cheap way into an expensive-sounding institution, which is usually the more interesting one anyway.
