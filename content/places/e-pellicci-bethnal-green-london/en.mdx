Priamo Pellicci started working the counter here in [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Pellicci); after his death in 1931, his wife Elide raised seven children in the flat upstairs while running the café below — her initial is the chrome "E" still on the fascia. The building's [Art Deco vitrolite-glass frontage and marquetry interior earned Grade II listing in 2005](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101391332-e-pellicci-cafe-st-peters-ward), which means the room you sit in is legally protected, not just old.

The café's reputation for regulars with a past is real: [the Kray twins lived round the corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Pellicci) and are widely said to have used it as a local, and [Harry Styles filmed a 2022 music video here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Pellicci). None of that is why it's still worth a visit. Current owner Maria Pellicci — the fourth generation behind the counter, working alongside Nev and Anna — [received Italy's Order of the Star in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Pellicci) for fostering Anglo-Italian relations, and the family started a podcast, *Down the Caff*, recorded on-site in 2024. This is a working business still generating news, not a museum piece coasting on its plaque.

Google's own pricing data puts a meal at [£10-20 per person](https://www.google.com/maps/place/E+Pellicci/@51.5264757,-0.0634292,17z), and three dated [r/fryup](https://www.reddit.com/r/fryup/comments/1qhj93q/e_pellicci_bethnal_green_london_1420/) posts tracking the full English specifically — £13.40, then £14.20, then £17, spread across roughly a year — back up what regulars already say: prices have climbed noticeably as the café's profile has grown, and the queue is real on weekends. One recent visitor called it hands-down the best fry-up they'd had; another, in the same thread of posts, called the hype "not worth it" at the current price. Both can be true of the same plate.

[Nev and Anna answered questions directly in a Reddit AMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/london/) — worth reading if you want the family's own account of running a set filmed as often as it's eaten in. Chips are cut by hand; ask for anything and they'll tell you why it's made that way.
