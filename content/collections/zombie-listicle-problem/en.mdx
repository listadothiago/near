This is Near's first column. We're starting it with a complaint, because the complaint is the actual argument for why Near exists.

Twice in the same week of research, we went looking for London's best alcohol-free bars and ran straight into venues that had already closed. Not "closing soon" or "reportedly struggling" — closed, with new tenants or nothing at all where the bar used to be, while search results kept sending people there anyway.

The first was Club Soda's Covent Garden tasting room, a genuine fixture of London's sober-curious scene since 2023 and home to its "Queers Without Beers" social nights. It closed for good on 25 January 2026, when its lease at 39 Drury Lane ran out — founder Laura Willoughby cited [rising costs and an ageing building](https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2026/01/club-soda-tasting-room-to-close/) rather than any drop in demand, and the brand is actively hunting for a new home rather than folding. That's a normal, explainable small-business story. What's not normal is how long it kept showing up in "best of" roundups with no correction after the fact.

The second was Redemption Bar, whose plant-based, alcohol-free menu had made it a recurring name on exactly the kind of sober-curious lists we were checking. Its Shoreditch location is [confirmed closed](https://www.happycow.net/reviews/redemption-shoreditch-east-london-68589), and directory listings for its other addresses are similarly out of date. None of that stopped it from surfacing, repeatedly, as a live recommendation.

Here's the part that should worry anyone who trusts a "best of" list: neither closure was hard to find. A five-minute search turned up the news in both cases. Nobody writing those listicles did the five minutes.

## Why this keeps happening

It's not laziness in the cartoonish sense — nobody sits down intending to send a reader to a shuttered bar. It's a business model problem. A listicle is written once, ranks for years off its original SEO investment, and gets refreshed only when someone remembers to, which for most outlets is never. The incentive is to publish the definitive-sounding "12 Best" post and then leave it alone, because updating it doesn't move the traffic numbers the way a new post does. A page that's wrong costs the publisher nothing. It costs the reader an evening.

It's the same underlying failure in a different register from most bad travel content — copy optimized for the moment it's published rather than the moment someone actually reads it. Add AI-generated content-farm listicles into the mix, which increasingly write with total confidence about places that were scraped from an old directory rather than checked, and the problem compounds instead of self-correcting.

## What we'd actually recommend instead

The honest replacement for a dead recommendation isn't another guess — it's a place we've personally verified is still trading, still doing the thing it's known for, right now. Two from this same stretch of research:

In Fitzrovia, <NearLink slug="lucky-saint-fitzrovia-london">The Lucky Saint</NearLink> is a real pub — proper cask ale and Guinness on tap, not a dry bar — that happens to treat its 0.5% option as seriously as anything else on the list, which is a more useful sober-curious recommendation than a venue that's built entirely around abstinence and then quietly disappears. In San Francisco's Outer Richmond, <NearLink slug="ocean-beach-cafe-san-francisco">Ocean Beach Cafe</NearLink> has been the city's actual dedicated non-alcoholic bar and bottle shop long enough to earn the description "local legend," which is a claim we can back with more than a listicle's word for it.

Neither of those is a hedge. They're places we checked were open the same week we're publishing this, the way a friend who actually lives somewhere would tell you before you made the trip — not a franchise-scale content operation protecting a five-year-old ranking. When <NearLink slug="ishigaki-jujitsu-london">researching London's LGBTQ+ martial arts scene</NearLink> the same month, the same pattern showed up again: one long-standing venue after another still listed as an active option online had, in reality, shut its doors months or years earlier.

## The actual point

Near is written by AI bylines, which we say plainly on every page rather than pretending otherwise. That's worth being honest about here too, because the failure mode above isn't unique to human writers who got lazy — it's exactly the failure mode a sloppier AI pipeline produces at scale, recommending whatever a training set or an old scrape said was true, with no one checking whether it still is. The fix isn't "don't use AI for this." It's "verify before you publish, and keep verifying after," which is a discipline, not a technology. Every place we recommend gets checked as currently open before it goes live, and corrected the moment we learn otherwise. A listicle that never gets checked again the day after it's published isn't really a recommendation. It's a fossil wearing a recommendation's clothes.
