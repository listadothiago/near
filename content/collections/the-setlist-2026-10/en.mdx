Most "festival preview" pieces run after the tickets are gone and the good rooms are booked. This one doesn't have that problem: [Primavera Sound São Paulo](https://www.ingresse.com/primavera-sound-sp-2026/) just confirmed its full day-by-day lineup, and 5–6 December 2026 is far enough out that you can actually do something about it — book the flight, sort the hotel, get the ticket before the good tiers sell through. Real dates, checked against the festival's own listings, published early enough to matter.

## What's confirmed, and when

Two days at Autódromo de Interlagos, São Paulo's Formula 1 circuit turned festival ground for the weekend:

- **Saturday, 5 December 2026** — The Strokes headline, with FKA twigs, Lily Allen, CMAT, and Courtney Barnett filling out a day that's doing a lot of "guitar band who actually means it" work.
- **Sunday, 6 December 2026** — Gorillaz close it out, alongside Arca, Yung Lean, and a run of acts leaning harder electronic than day one.

Forty acts total across the weekend. I'm not going to pretend I have opinions on all forty — I don't, yet — but The Strokes and Gorillaz on the same bill, three months out, with dates locked and pre-sale already run (BB Visa cardholders got first crack 23 July, general sale opened 28 July), is exactly the kind of thing worth a column entry the day it's confirmable rather than the week of.

## While you're planning the trip: a real room to catch a show closer to home

I cover the world, not just the headline acts, so here's the local half of this issue. If you're anywhere near São Paulo's coast before December, <NearLink slug="teatro-clube-da-eskyna-santos">Teatro Clube da Eskyna</NearLink> in Santos's Centro Histórico is a genuine nightlife-sound venue worth knowing about on its own terms — not a Primavera Sound satellite stage, nothing that grand, just a real room where a real scene happens on a normal week. I'm not going to claim any Interlagos act is turning up there; that's not how this works, and pretending otherwise is exactly the kind of thing this column exists to avoid. What I will say: if you're building a music-focused stretch of a São Paulo state trip around the December festival, it's worth checking what's on there in the weeks before you fly out, rather than treating live music as a thing that only happens on a festival weekend.
