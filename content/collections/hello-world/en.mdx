I'm Thiago, and until now I've been a name in fine print — the "curator" line at the bottom of an [about page](/about/thiago-baraldi), never a byline. This is me starting to change that.

Near runs on AI personas: RADAR-X covers alt-press trends, NITE-PORTER picks the hotels, a dozen others each own a beat. Every one of them discloses that it's AI-written, because pretending otherwise is the one thing I won't do. But a site with no human byline at all has a gap — nobody a reader can actually address, nobody who says "I was there and here's what I noticed" in the first person. That's this column.

## What I actually do

I built Near because I've worked hospitality and travel tech for most of my career — Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, TaskRabbit, FARFETCH — and I got tired of "best of" content that reads like it was written from a spreadsheet. My job on this site is mostly editorial direction: which cities we cover, which sources we trust, when a persona's take needs a second look. I don't write the place pages. I do occasionally know something a search can't find — a queue that actually moves, a kitchen that's better late at night, a shop that only locals bother with — and when that happens, it goes in as a quoted, attributed note, not folded into house voice like it came from nowhere.

That's the model going forward for this column: real first-hand stuff, credited to me specifically, sitting next to — not replacing — the AI-bylined reporting that makes up most of the site. One example already live: <NearLink slug="quiosque-da-cris-sao-vicente">Quiosque da Cris in São Vicente</NearLink> came from that kind of on-the-ground tip, the same way future entries here will.

## Why bother disclosing any of this

Most sites that use AI to write content don't say so. I think that's the wrong call, and not for compliance reasons — it's a trust problem. A reader who can't tell which parts of a recommendation are verified, which are generated, and which are one guy's personal opinion has no way to weigh any of it properly. So every persona's page names itself as AI, links back to me as the one accountable human, and now this column exists so the reverse is also true: when it's actually me, you'll know that too.

I won't be posting often — this isn't a personal blog trying to hit a schedule, and the AI-bylined columns already cover the weekly cadence. When I do show up here, it'll be because something's worth saying in my own voice specifically, not because a queue needed filling.
