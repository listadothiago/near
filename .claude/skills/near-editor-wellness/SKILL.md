# near-editor-wellness

The wellness/fitness advisor lens for `near-editor` — the "practitioner
lens" the main style guide already names in "Categories, tags, and
'advisor lenses.'" Not a separate voice; this sharpens Near's one voice
for the `wellness-fitness` beat the same way `near-editor-gastronomic`
does for `food-drink`.

## The core discipline: what you'd actually do, and who's running it

"Great studio" is a category. "Vinyasa classes run 75 minutes, the
teacher corrects hands-on if you're okay with it, and the room is heated
to a specific, named temperature" is a thing. Every wellness/fitness
piece should be able to name:

- **The actual activity/format** — the specific class type, training
  style, or modality (not just "yoga" but the style; not just "gym" but
  what kind of training the space is actually set up for — powerlifting
  platforms, a boxing ring, a reformer Pilates studio).
- **Who teaches/runs it and how**, when the source supports it — a
  named instructor's approach, a training philosophy, whether it's
  drop-in or membership-only, group class vs. one-on-one.
- **The practical texture of showing up** — walk-in vs. booking
  required, gear provided or bring-your-own, locker/shower situation,
  a real sense of who's actually in the room (beginners welcome and
  genuinely accommodated, or a serious/competitive room where a
  first-timer might feel out of place — say which, honestly).

Don't invent a class format or a instructor detail the source doesn't
support — same non-negotiable honesty rule as everywhere else in Near.

## Price and commitment, honestly

Drop-in price, membership structure, whether there's a genuinely
beginner-friendly on-ramp or the room skews toward people who already
know what they're doing — carry this forward plainly when a source gives
real signal, the same way the gastronomic lens treats price.

## Where this overlaps `near-editor-sports`

The boundary: **wellness-fitness** is personal practice/self-care —
somewhere you go on your own to work out, stretch, recover (a gym, a
yoga studio, a spa, a climbing gym used solo). **near-editor-sports**
covers joinable, social, participatory activity — a team you can join, a
rental, a class that's really about a group activity or a sport rather
than solo fitness (a rugby team, a kayak rental, a martial-arts school
where the point is sparring partners and belts, not just a workout).
Some places genuinely straddle both (a martial-arts gym that's both a
fitness practice and a competitive team) — let both lenses inform the
same piece rather than forcing an artificial split; see `near-editor-
gastronomic`'s note on the same kind of category overlap for the pattern
to follow.

## Red flags to push back on

- "A great place to get fit," "amazing energy," "life-changing" with
  nothing concrete underneath.
- No mention of what an actual first visit looks like — schedule, cost,
  what to bring — when a source has that information available.
- Praise with no source backing it, exactly as much a problem here as
  anywhere else in Near.
