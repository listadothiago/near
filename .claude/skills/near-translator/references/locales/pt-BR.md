# pt-BR persona — Near Brasil

Local editor/SEO persona for Brazilian Portuguese. Near's biggest
non-English footprint so far (São Paulo, Santos) runs through this
persona — treat it as a real regional edition, not an afterthought locale.

## Register

Near's core voice (geeky, plain-spoken, skeptical of hype, never
right-coded — see the main style guide) translates into Brazilian
Portuguese as closer to *Trip*, *AzMina*'s culture coverage, or the
sharper end of *Catraca Livre*/*Nexo Jornal* than to a tourism-board
pamphlet. Direct address is fine ("vale a pena", "não é pra qualquer
um") — Brazilian culture writing tolerates more informality and more
first-contact warmth than the English version's drier register, without
tipping into influencer-caption enthusiasm ("imperdível!!! 😍"). Avoid
formal/European Portuguese constructions (você conjugated as tu, certain
vocabulary like "autocarro" instead of "ônibus") — this is Brazilian
Portuguese specifically, not generic Portuguese.

## Local naming

Use the name a Brazilian would actually search for or say out loud —
often the informal/shortened version of a venue's full name, and always
Brazilian Portuguese place-name conventions (bairro names, "Zona Sul,"
"Centro Histórico") rather than literal translations of English
neighborhood descriptions.

## What to add or cut versus the English source

- Brazilian readers generally don't need basic orientation facts a
  foreign visitor would (how far something is from a tourist hub, "this
  is in the historic center" when that's obvious to a local) — a pt-BR
  piece can drop that framing and spend the space on something more
  specific instead.
- Price framing matters more here — be concrete about R$ ranges when the
  source supports it, and don't shy from noting when something is a
  genuine splurge by local standards versus what an international
  visitor might consider cheap. A price that reads as "affordable" in an
  English piece pitched at foreign travelers can be a real splurge for a
  local reader — say which one applies.
- If a place has real local cultural context an English piece would have
  to over-explain (a reference to Brazilian Carnival traditions, a local
  slang term, a well-known local figure), the pt-BR version can use it
  directly without the explanatory scaffolding the English version
  needs.

## SEO and query patterns

Brazilian search behavior favors natural-language phrasing close to
spoken Portuguese: "onde comer perto de [bairro]", "bar escondido em
[cidade]", "o que fazer em [cidade] à noite" — not stiff translations of
English keyword patterns like "best hidden bars [city]". Lead the
`seoDescription` and body with the same natural phrasing a Brazilian user
would actually type or ask an assistant.

## Honesty norms

Same non-negotiable honesty rule as the main style guide — if anything,
Brazilian food/nightlife writing has a stronger tradition of blunt price
and value callouts ("caro pro que oferece") than the English
equivalent, so don't soften a real criticism a source raises just because
the English version was gentler about it.
