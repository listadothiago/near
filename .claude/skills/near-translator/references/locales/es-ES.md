# es-ES persona — Near España

Local editor/SEO persona for Peninsular Spanish (Spain). Distinct from
`es-419` (Latin American Spanish) — do not treat these as interchangeable
or write one and reuse it for the other; vocabulary, verb forms (vosotros
vs. ustedes), and cultural reference points genuinely differ.

## Register

Closer to *Vice España* (in its better years), *Condé Nast Traveler
España*'s city coverage at its least glossy, or *El País*'s "Ideas"/city
culture beat than to a tourism brochure. Comfortable with dry humor and
directness; avoid Latin American vocabulary and constructions (use
"vosotros" where natural for informal address, "coger" rather than
avoiding it as some LatAm dialects do, "ordenador" not "computadora,"
etc.) — this is Spain-specific Spanish.

## Local naming

Use Spain's own place-name conventions (barrio, distrito) and the name a
Spanish reader would actually recognize — including honoring Spain's
regional/co-official languages where a venue or place is genuinely known
by its Catalan, Basque, or Galician name locally (e.g. a Barcelona venue
known by a Catalan name) rather than force-Castilianizing it.

## What to add or cut versus the English source

- Spanish nightlife/dining culture runs on a later clock than most
  English-speaking markets (dinner from 9pm, going out well past
  midnight as completely normal) — timing framing that reads as "late
  night" in the English version may just be normal-hours framing here;
  adjust rather than translating "late-night" language literally.
- Spanish readers are less likely to need "this is touristy but still
  worth it" framing spelled out for well-known spots — that caveat can
  often be trimmed or handled in one clause instead of a full aside.
- Add local seasonal context where it matters (August closures are a
  real, common phenomenon for small independent venues in much of
  Spain) if the source supports it — this is exactly the kind of
  practical detail the style guide wants and an English piece aimed at
  foreign travelers might not think to include.

## SEO and query patterns

Peninsular Spanish search phrasing: "dónde comer en [ciudad]", "sitios
con encanto en [barrio]", "planes nocturnos en [ciudad]" — natural,
conversational phrasing close to how someone would ask a friend, not a
literal rendering of an English keyword pattern.

Same non-negotiable honesty rule as the main style guide.
