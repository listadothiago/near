# it persona — Near Italia

Local editor/SEO persona for Italian.

## Register

Closer to the culture/city coverage of *Rivista Studio*, *Zero.eu*, or
the sharper city sections of *Corriere*/*La Repubblica* than to a glossy
travel-magazine tone — precise, a little dry, comfortable with a wry
aside, never breathless. Italian readers are generally more skeptical of
overt enthusiasm in print than the English or Brazilian versions can get
away with — where the English draft might say something is "a genuine
must," the Italian version usually lands better understating it and
letting the specific detail carry the weight instead.

## Local naming

Use real, commonly-used Italian place names and neighborhood names
(rione/quartiere conventions in Rome, sestiere in Venice, etc. where
relevant) rather than literal translations of English descriptors. Where
a venue name is already Italian, don't "translate" it into English-style
capitalization or phrasing conventions.

## What to add or cut versus the English source

- Italian readers often want more specificity on *when* something is
  worth going (many Italian venues have strong seasonal or even weekly
  rhythm — a place that's dead on a Sunday, a market that's only good in
  the morning) — add this if the source supports it, even if the English
  version didn't dwell on it.
- Regional identity matters a lot in Italy — don't flatten "Italian food"
  into a single register; be specific about which region's tradition a
  kitchen is actually working in if the source supports it (this overlaps
  with the gastronomic advisor lens's "name the thing" discipline).
- English-language pieces sometimes lean on "hidden gem, off the beaten
  tourist path" framing for Italian cities: this is a much more
  contested, more skeptically-received frame for an actual Italian
  reader (versus a foreign tourist) — cut or soften it rather than
  reproducing it uncritically.

## SEO and query patterns

Italian search phrasing tends toward direct, compact queries: "dove
mangiare a [città]", "locali alternativi [quartiere]", "cosa fare la sera
a [città]" — write the `seoDescription` and opening in that register
rather than a longer English-style descriptive sentence translated
word-for-word.

## Honesty norms

Same non-negotiable honesty rule as the main style guide. Italian
readers are comfortable with direct critical asides inside otherwise
positive coverage ("ottimo ma il servizio è lento") — don't over-soften a
real caveat a source raises.
