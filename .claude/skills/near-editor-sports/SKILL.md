# near-editor-sports

The participatory-sports advisor lens for `near-editor`. Covers activity
that's fundamentally social/organized: something you join, rent, or show
up to as part of a group — a team, a class built around a sport (not
solo fitness), a rental outfit, a league.

See `near-editor-wellness`'s own note on the boundary: wellness-fitness
is personal practice (a gym, a solo yoga session); this lens is for
activity that's inherently about other people — teammates, a class
cohort, a rental transaction with real practical logistics.

## The core discipline: can a reader actually join or do this?

Every piece in this lens should answer the reader's real, practical
question — "how would I actually do this?" — not just describe that the
activity exists nearby:

- **How to join/book**, concretely: a team's tryout or open-practice
  process, a rental outfit's pricing and what's included (gear,
  instruction), a class's schedule and skill-level expectations.
- **Skill level and welcome-ness, honestly** — beginner-friendly and
  genuinely says so, or a competitive/experienced-only room, stated
  plainly rather than assumed.
- **What makes this specific team/school/outfit worth choosing** over a
  generic version of the same activity — a particular training lineage
  (for martial arts especially — name the actual style/school
  tradition, not just "martial arts classes"), a team's actual league
  and competitive level, a rental spot's specific put-in location and
  water conditions.

## LGBTQIA+ teams and leagues — name them specifically when they exist

Per the operator's standing interest in LGBTQIA+ coverage: when a sport
has a genuine gay/queer-specific team or league in a market Near covers
(gay rugby, queer run clubs, LGBTQIA+ sports leagues), that's worth
surfacing explicitly and specifically — not folded into a generic "and
it's inclusive" aside. Same honesty rule applies: only if a source
actually confirms the team/league is real and currently active.

## Consulting other lenses

- A martial-arts school that's also clearly someone's serious fitness
  practice → also draw on `near-editor-wellness`'s practitioner-lens
  discipline for the training-format details.
- An outdoor rental (kayak, paddleboard) → coordinate with
  `near-editor-outdoors` rather than duplicating; outdoors covers the
  setting/nature angle, sports covers the joining/booking logistics.

## Red flags to push back on

- "Great for sports fans" or "an active lifestyle" with no actual sport,
  team, or booking path named.
- Listing an activity type (e.g. "rugby") without naming the actual club
  or league — that's not concrete enough for a reader to act on.
