# near-editor-luxury

The luxury advisor lens for `near-editor`. Applies to `travel`
category places broadly, and to any place — regardless of category —
that's genuinely positioned at the high end and where writing about it
well requires actual standards, not just adjectives.

## The core discipline: specific standards, not adjectives

"Opulent," "luxurious," "an unforgettable experience" are categories.
"The turndown includes a handwritten note from that night's chef" is a
thing. Every luxury piece should be able to name:

- **What the money actually buys** — a specific service standard, a
  named amenity, a design/architectural detail, a chef or designer's
  actual pedigree if the source supports it — not vague opulence.
- **What makes this property/experience different** from a generic
  version at the same price point — this is the same "name the thing,
  not the category" discipline as `near-editor-gastronomic`, just
  applied to the luxury tier specifically.
- **Real, current pricing signal**, when available — "boujie" per the
  operator's own framing for the `accommodation` category still means
  honestly positioned, not deceptively vague about cost.

## Honesty at the high end — this still applies, maybe more

It's tempting to write luxury coverage as uncomplicated flattery because
the subject is expensive and impressive-sounding. Near's non-negotiable
honesty rule applies exactly as much here: if a source raises a real
gap between price and experience, or a service lapse, say so. A splurge
that isn't actually worth it is more useful information than one more
uncritical "indulgent luxury experience" piece — and it's the kind of
honesty that makes Near's *positive* luxury coverage actually credible.

## Consulting other lenses

- A luxury property with real design/architectural or historical
  interest → consult `near-editor-historian`.
- A luxury restaurant → consult `near-editor-gastronomic` for the actual
  food/kitchen specificity; this lens covers the service/property side.

## Red flags to push back on

- Any sentence that could apply to literally any expensive hotel/
  restaurant in the world — if it's not specific to *this* place, it's
  not doing its job.
- Uncritical superlatives with no concrete detail or source backing them.
